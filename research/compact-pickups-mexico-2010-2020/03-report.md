# Compact pickups in Mexico, 2010–2020: sales leaders, specs, used prices, and cheap-but-reliable trade-offs

## Executive Summary

Mexico’s compact pickup class in 2010–2020 was a small set of **front-wheel-drive, gasoline, unibody** trucks — not the body-on-frame work pickups that dominate national charts. The nameplates that actually filled that class were **Chevrolet Tornado**, **Volkswagen Saveiro**, **Fiat Strada** then **RAM 700**, and from May 2018 **Renault Oroch** (which Renault called a Sport Utility Pickup, not a work midsize). There were almost never five compact models on sale at once, so a literal “top 5 per year” is usually **three trucks, four after Oroch**. [1][2][8][15]

Where full INEGI-based model lists exist (**2018–2020**), the compact ranking is:

- **2018:** RAM 700, Saveiro, Tornado, Oroch (partial year). [5]
- **2019:** Saveiro, Oroch, RAM 700, Tornado — the only documented year RAM 700 was not compact #1. [6][4]
- **2020:** RAM 700, Saveiro, Oroch, Tornado. [4][7]

**Cheap but mechanically simple** still points at **Tornado** (lowest 2026 residual, 1.8 / 105 hp / five-speed only, 734 kg). **Parts/volume** points at **first-gen RAM 700** or **Saveiro**. **Cabin and the only automatic** point at **Oroch**. None of the 2020 compact four were on Motorpasión’s “buy with ESC” side of the ledger; Latin NCAP later gave the **2021** RAM 700/Strada **one star**, which does not score the 2015–2020 1.6 truck and does not make the new one a crash-safety bargain. [11][16][17][9]

Used values observed in August 2026 guides and ads are **asking/guide pesos, not notarized sales**: work cabs often **about $120,000–$180,000**; Club Cab / Pepper / Oroch often **about $190,000–$260,000** on the guide, with dealer asks frequently higher. [18][19][20][21]

## Introduction

This report is for a used-truck decision in Mexico: which compact pickups sold new in **2010–2020** were the volume leaders, what engines and gearboxes they had, what they ask for now, and which look **cheap but reliable** — with the case against treating the popular compact as a miniature NP300.

**In scope:** Tornado, Saveiro, Strada/RAM 700, Oroch, and any confirmed compact peer. **Out of scope as “compact”:** Nissan NP300/Frontier, Toyota Hilux, Ford Ranger, Mitsubishi L200, Chevrolet S10/Colorado, VW Amarok, and full-size trucks. Those appear only as contrast, because official “pickup” charts are not compact charts. [3][4]

## The compact class in Mexico, 2010–2020

Mexican specialist press treated “pickups compactas / pequeñas” as **small, usually FWD, car-based pickups**. In November 2020 Autocosmos called that market “very reduced” and named **only three exemplars then on sale: Saveiro, Tornado and RAM 700**. [1] When RAM 700 launched as a 2015 model it was described as competing **directly with Tornado and Saveiro**. [2]

**Renault Oroch** is the classification dispute. It reached dealers in **May 2018**. Renault (via Mexican used-car press) branded it a **SUP** sitting between Saveiro/RAM 700 and NP300/L200. The same Autocosmos site nevertheless ran a compact-pickup comparison of Oroch vs RAM 700 later that November. This report therefore ranks Oroch from 2018 as **compact-adjacent**, and records that Autocosmos’s “only three” line omitted it. [8][15][9]

**Documented presence (not a sales rank):**

| Years | Compact / compact-adjacent nameplates |
| --- | --- |
| 2010 | Tornado; Saveiro; Fiat Strada; Ford Courier still cited in the small-pickup set |
| 2011–2013 | Tornado; Saveiro; Fiat Strada |
| 2014 | Tornado; Saveiro; Strada, with RAM 700 from October (Strada rebadge) |
| 2015–2017 | Tornado; Saveiro; RAM 700 |
| 2018–2020 | Tornado; Saveiro; RAM 700; Oroch from May 2018. Second-generation RAM 700 presented **November 2020**. [1][2][8] |

GM Mexico said Tornado held **55% of the small-pickup category** at year-end 2010. [10] Volkswagen later claimed Saveiro had **44%** of its segment in 2014 (quoted in the press) and **more than 44%** again in its 2019 commercial-vehicle release. [12][13]

**Contrast:** T21, citing INEGI, reported **193,332** pickup sales in 2019. **NP300** alone was **62,989**. NP300, Hilux, Ranger Crew Cab, L200 and Tacoma were **52.74%** of all pickups. None of those five is this compact class. [3]

## Engines, horsepower, and transmissions (Mexico)

All of these compact pickups in the fetched Mexican launches are **gasoline, FWD, unibody**. Payloads cluster around **650–750 kg**. No diesel appeared in those launches.

| Model | Years in this window | Engine | Power / torque | Transmission | Payload (sourced) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chevrolet Tornado | through 2020 | 1.8 L I4 | 105 hp @ 5,400 rpm / 119 lb-ft @ 3,000 rpm | 5-speed manual only | 734 kg [10][11] |
| Volkswagen Saveiro (2017 Mexican range) | throughout the decade; 2017 specs below | 1.6 L I4 | 101 hp / 105 lb-ft (8-valve Starline/Highline/Trendline); **110 hp / 113 lb-ft** (16-valve Cross) | 5-speed manual | Regular cab up to 712 kg; extended 696 kg; double cab 667 kg; Cross 607 kg [25] |
| RAM 700 first gen | 2015 through most of 2020 | **1.6 L I4** | **115 hp / 119 lb-ft** | 5-speed manual | Regular cab 705 kg; Club Cab 650 kg [2] |
| RAM 700 second gen | from November 2020 | 1.3 L Firefly I4 | 98 hp / 95 lb-ft | 5-speed manual only at launch | Regular 750 kg; crew 650 kg; tow 400 kg [1] |
| Renault Oroch | from May 2018 | 2.0 L I4 (Duster family) | 140 hp / 142–144 lb-ft (one used-car piece says **143 hp**) | **6-speed manual or 4-speed automatic** | 650 kg; tow 710 kg in the 2020 compare [8][9][15] |

**Tornado.** Dealer and 2011.5 launch coverage agree on the 1.8 / 105 hp / five-speed formula, ABS and dual front airbags on later trucks, claimed combined fuel use about **15.5–16 km/l** depending on trim. **No automatic** in these Mexican sources. [10][11]

**Saveiro 2017.** New prices from **$182,000** (Starline) to **$327,000** (Cross). From 2017 Autocosmos said all versions had four-wheel discs, ABS, hydraulic steering and two airbags; **Cross** added ESC/traction, locking differential and hill-start assist. [25]

**RAM 700 first generation.** Launch prices Regular Cab **$189,900**, Club Cab **$249,900**. Combined consumption quoted **12.38 km/l**. Club Cab had a **front differential lock**. Autocosmos’s drive: the 1.6 needs **more than 4,000 rpm** for strong acceleration. [2] A later RAM technical PDF lists **1.4 Fire (84 hp)** and 1.3 Firefly (98 hp). The **Mexican 2015 launch specifies 1.6 / 115 hp**. This report does not overwrite the Mexican launch with that PDF. [2]

**RAM 700 2021 (November 2020).** Combined **18.3 km/l**; ESC, traction control, hill-start assist; two airbags on regular cab, four on crew cab. New prices SLT Regular **$244,900** through Laramie **$334,900**. That 98 hp Firefly is **less power** than the 2015 1.6. [1]

**Oroch.** Independent **multilink rear** suspension vs leaf-sprung RAM 700. Launch: ZEN 6-speed **$269,900**; INTENS 4-speed auto **$313,900**; OUTSIDER 4-speed auto **$328,900**. By February 2020 Seminuevos listed ZEN **$280,500** / Outsider TM **$322,700** / Outsider TA **$343,100**. Combined consumption in the 2020 Autocosmos compare: **16.1 km/l** manual, **14.8 km/l** automatic. [8][9][15]

Mexican fichas for **2010 Courier** and **2010–2014 Strada** were not retrieved.

## Reliability, safety, and known problems

Independent Mexican **breakdown-rate** series for these trucks were not found. What is documented is **safety specification**, crash tests on a **later** RAM 700, and product limits.

### Missing ESC (high confidence for 2020 showroom spec)

In January 2020 Motorpasión México listed **Chevrolet Tornado, RAM 700, Renault Oroch and Volkswagen Saveiro** among models then sold **without electronic stability control** (NP300 was listed as ESC optional). Latin NCAP’s standing advice, which that article followed, is not to buy cars without ESC. [16]

That is **2020 showroom spec**. Autocosmos’s November 2020 RAM 700 **2021** launch lists ESC as part of the **new** generation, which is consistent with first-gen 700s as commonly sold in Mexico lacking it. Saveiro **Cross** already had ESC in the 2017 launch; base Saveiro did not in that article. Motorpasión’s model-level “Saveiro” line is coarser than the Cross exception. [1][25][16]

### Latin NCAP: 2021 RAM 700 / Fiat Strada, not the 2015–2020 1.6

In October 2022 Latin NCAP tested the **second-generation** Strada/RAM 700 and awarded **one star** for both single- and double-cab. Global NCAP’s summary: **unstable structure and footwell** in the frontal test; side airbags (double cab) deployed incorrectly and were small; almost no assistance systems meeting the protocol (no AEB, lane support, blind-spot detection, or speed limiter). The tested trucks **did** have ESC as standard. [17]

That result **does not** rate 2015–2020 1.6 RAM 700, Tornado, Saveiro or Oroch. It still matters for late-2020 buyers of the new generation. The older 700 had **less** standard assist equipment than the truck that already scored one star. [16][1][17]

No Latin NCAP result for Mexican Tornado, Saveiro or Oroch 2010–2020 was used here.

### Mechanical “reliability”

Specialist press describes these as **urban/light-commercial** tools: Saveiro as a durability/efficiency example, RAM 700 as a versatile compact, Oroch as the more SUV-like option **not** positioned as a hard work truck. That is journalism, not a failure-rate study. [26][8][15]

**Recalls.** No PROFECO or manufacturer bulletin set for 2010–2020 VINs was opened. Absence of a fetched recall is **not** a clean bill of health.

**Use-case.** Payloads are **607–750 kg**. Using a compact FWD unibody as if it were an NP300 is a foreseeable misuse; no official Mexican overload-failure statistic was found. [25][2][11][8]

## Cheap but reliable: advantages and problems

No independent Mexican TCO study comparing these four was found. VW’s 2019 release is the only TCO line in the set, and it is a **manufacturer claim** that Saveiro led its segment in sales **and** TCO. [13]

“Reliable” here splits into **simplicity** (small naturally aspirated four, manual gearbox, cheap residual) versus **crash avoidance** (ESC, structure). Those two rankings do not agree.

### Chevrolet Tornado — cheapest documented residual

**Advantages:** Guía Autométrica 2015 about **$120,000–$135,000**, 2018 about **$148,000–$171,000**; simple 1.8 / five-speed; **highest compact payload sourced (734 kg)**; Chevrolet commercial network; claimed ~16 km/l. [18][19][11][10]

**Problems:** no automatic; **2020 spec without ESC**; sales faded (**8,034** in 2011 → **5,246** in 2018 → **3,717** in 2019 → **2,808** in 2020). Lower late-cycle volume is an **inference** about thinner healthy used stock and weaker long-run parts continuity, not a parts-catalog citation. Cheap residuals can also mean high miles and fleet use. [24][5][4][16]

### RAM 700 (first gen) and Saveiro — volume / parts proxy

**RAM 700 advantages:** compact unit leader in 2015–2018 and 2020; Regular and Club Cab; 2015 **1.6 / 115 hp**; 650–705 kg; ABS/airbags; 7-year/100,000 km powertrain warranty when new; front locker on Club Cab. Used Club Cabs in this session often **about $205,000–$270,000**. [23][14][5][7][2][27]

**RAM 700 problems:** first-gen **no ESC** on the 2020 Motorpasión list; **2021** nameplate **one star** at Latin NCAP; 1.6 wants high rpm; FWD leaf springs; 2021 engine is **98 hp**. Autométrica residuals for 700 were not retrieved. [16][17][2][1]

**Saveiro advantages:** VW **44%** segment claims (2014 press quote and 2019 primary); **2019 compact sales #1 (8,890)**; 2017 range from **$182,000**, 101–110 hp, 607–712 kg; discs/ABS/airbags across the 2017 range; Cross had ESC; work-cab 2026 guide **about $132,000–$162,000**. [12][13][6][25][20]

**Saveiro problems:** base trucks on the 2020 no-ESC list; double-cab payload falls versus regular cab; Cross payload is the **lowest (607 kg)**. [16][25]

### Renault Oroch — only automatic, 140 hp, five-seat cabin

**Advantages:** 2.0 / ~140 hp; **6-speed manual or 4-speed auto**; five-door SUV cabin; multilink rear; 2019 compact **#2 (7,527)**; 2026 guide about **$177,000–$220,000**. [8][6][21]

**Problems:** dearest when new (**$270k–$329k** in 2018, higher by 2020); Renault’s own positioning: **not** NP300/L200; worse fuel than 2021 RAM 700 in Autocosmos’s compare (**16.1 / 14.8 vs 18.3 km/l**); two airbags, no ESC in that 2020 compare; still **650 kg**. Ads often **above** the guide. [8][15][9][16][21]

## Counter-case: when the popular compact is the wrong tool

1. **Mexico’s real volume pickup is not compact.** In 2019 NP300 sold **62,989** against Saveiro **8,890** and RAM 700 **7,483**. AMDA’s Guillermo Rosales tied early-2020 pickup resilience partly to **Guardia Nacional and CFE** fleet purchases — not 650 kg FWD coupés. [3][4]

2. **Payload is about half a work midsize.** Tornado 734 kg; Saveiro 607–712 kg; RAM 700 650–705 kg (2015) or 650–750 kg (2021); Oroch 650 kg. Renault explicitly kept Oroch out of the NP300/L200 bin. [11][25][2][1][15]

3. **“RAM 700 is the safe compact” fails the crash file.** First-gen 700s sat on the 2020 no-ESC list. The 2021 truck added ESC and still scored **one star**, with an unstable structure. A used 2015–2020 700 is **less equipped** than the truck that already failed to five-star. [16][17][1]

4. **Sales leadership is not uptime.** RAM 700’s unit lead shows price and cab options. The 2021 1.3 is **weaker** than the 2015 1.6. [2][1]

5. **Saveiro’s 44% share does not erase spec holes.** Two airbags as the 2017 range kit; only Cross listed with ESC; Cross is the lightest payload. [25][16]

6. **Tornado is cheap because it was exiting.** Share leader in 2010, compact standout in 2011, **fourth of four** by 2020. No source here says the 1.8 is fragile; the counter-case is obsolescence, safety spec, and disposable work-mule use. [10][24][4]

7. **Oroch’s automatic is a trap if the job is payload.** Five doors and 140 hp feel “more truck”; 650 kg and Renault’s SUP label say otherwise. [15][9]

8. **For proven volume plus body-on-frame work, INEGI tables point outside this segment** — NP300, then Hilux/Ranger — which are **not compact and not cheapest**. [3][4]

## Synthesis / Conclusion

**What sources report.** The compact class in Mexico 2010–2020 was three (then four) FWD gasoline unibody pickups. Complete compact podiums exist for **2018–2020**. RAM 700 led most of those years; **2019 belonged to Saveiro**, with Oroch second. Specs are stable: Tornado 1.8/105/5MT; Saveiro 1.6/101–110/5MT; first-gen 700 1.6/115/5MT; Oroch 2.0/140 with **the only automatic**; second-gen 700 1.3/98 from November 2020. 2026 work-cab residuals start near **$120,000**; crew/Club/Oroch money is higher. The documented “problem” is **safety spec and payload**, not a published lemon rate. [5][6][7][2][25][8][1][18][16]

**What the available evidence suggests** (inference, not an extra source). For **cheap, simple, maximum compact payload**, a inspected **2015–2018 Tornado** is the peso-minimizing used buy, with eyes open on ESC, miles, and a fading nameplate. For **still-sold parts and a Club Cab**, a **first-gen RAM 700** or a **Saveiro work cab** is the volume choice; Cross Saveiro is the only compact in this set with **documented ESC in 2017**, at the cost of **607 kg**. For **an automatic and five seats**, Oroch is the only compact-adjacent answer, and it is the wrong answer if the job is NP300 work. If the job **is** NP300 work, the compact class is the wrong aisle.

Confidence is **high** on 2018–2020 ranks and the Mexican launch specs that were re-fetched; **medium** on 2026 pesos (guide/asking); **low** on mechanical failure frequency and on numeric compact ranks for 2010–2014.

## Limitations & Open Questions

- **No fifth compact** in most years; filling the slot with NP300/Hilux would answer a different question.
- **2010, 2012, 2013, 2014** lack a fetched full INEGI/AMIA compact unit table. 2015–2017 have RAM 700 units and only an upper bound for Saveiro and Tornado.
- Ford Courier’s last Mexican model year was not confirmed from a Ford de México primary (encyclopedic secondary excluded from the body).
- Direct INEGI RAIAVL microdata was not downloaded; 2018–2020 units are Motorpasión/Autocosmos reprints of INEGI.
- RAM 700 **1.4 Fire 84 hp** on a later PDF vs **1.6 / 115 hp** in the Mexican 2015 launch remains unresolved; this report keeps the Mexican launch for 2015–2020 gen 1.
- Oroch **140 vs 143 hp** and **142 vs 144 lb-ft** are press discrepancies.
- Saveiro hp before the 2017 Autocosmos article, and 2010–2014 Tornado tunes, were not separately sheeted.
- Latin NCAP 2022 does not rate 2015–2020 1.6 700, Tornado, Saveiro or Oroch. Related-car scores (Gol Trend, Duster) were excluded.
- No PROFECO recall series, J.D. Power IQS, theft ranking, or independent maintenance-cost survey.
- Guía Autométrica for **RAM 700** was not found; Seminuevos listing stats used instead. Saveiro 2018 Highline/Cross and Oroch 2020 Intens rows were missing from extracts.
- 2026 prices are **guide or asking**, not transacted; inflation-adjusted depreciation was not computed.
- Cumulative Tornado production (>81,000 since 2004 in one secondary) was not re-confirmed on the GM briefing page that was re-fetched; omitted from the body.
