# q01: How did Mexico define the compact/small pickup segment in 2010–2020, and which nameplates were actually sold in that class each year?

**Status:** researched — pending validation
**Source types to prioritize:** Manufacturer launches, Autocosmos segment explainers, AMDA/AMIA category notes

## Findings

Mexican press consistently treated “pickups compactas / pequeñas” as a **small, usually front-wheel-drive, car-based (unibody) pickup** class, not the body-on-frame work trucks that dominate national pickup charts. In late 2020 Autocosmos stated that Mexico’s compact pickup market was “very reduced” and named **only three exemplars then on sale: Volkswagen Saveiro, Chevrolet Tornado and RAM 700** [S1]. The same outlet, when RAM 700 launched for model-year 2015, said it would compete **directly against Chevrolet Tornado and Volkswagen Saveiro** [S2].

That three-nameplate core is the spine of 2010–2020. Earlier in the decade the class also included **Fiat Strada** (sold under its own badge before the RAM rebadge) and **Ford Courier**. An Autocosmos 2010 note on Brazil’s Peugeot Hoggar listed Mexico’s small-pickup set as **Chevrolet Tornado, Ford Courier and Fiat Strada** [S3]. Autocosmos later recalled Pointer Pickup, Ford Courier and Chevrolet Chevy Pickup as the older “essence” of the small work pickup [S4]. Independent secondary summaries state Courier **stopped being sold in Mexico after 2010** [S5]; this session did not retrieve a Ford Mexico press release confirming the exact last model year, so 2010 is treated as the last year Courier is documented in that Mexican small-pickup set.

**Chevrolet Tornado** (Brazil’s Chevrolet Montana) was the established small-pickup leader at the start of the window. GM Mexico said that at year-end 2010 Tornado held **55% of the small-pickup category**, and that more than **81,000** units had been sold from the start of commercialization in 2004 through early 2011 [S6][S7]. Motorpasión later described the then-current Tornado as on sale in Mexico **since 2011** with a 1.8-litre, 105 hp, five-speed-manual formula and payload in the mid-700 kg range, with a hoped-for larger successor around 2020 [S8]. Autocosmos’s 2020 calendar-year pickup table still lists Tornado (2,808 units in 2020; 3,717 in 2019), so the nameplate remained on sale through 2020 [S9]. Wikipedia’s Montana page (not used here as a sales source) is consistent with a Mexico discontinuation after 2020; the primary Mexican sales tables confirm it was still a 2020 retail model [S9].

**Volkswagen Saveiro** was the other continuous Brazilian compact. Volkswagen of Mexico’s commercial-vehicles division presented a double-cab Saveiro as complementing an already-strong compact, and a VW executive quoted in Motorpasión called **2014 “the year of the Saveiro,”** with **44% of the compact-pickup segment** [S10]. Autocosmos’s 2017 Saveiro launch said the model then held **32%** of its segment in Mexico and competed with RAM 700 and Tornado [S11]. VW’s 2019 year-end commercial-vehicle release again claimed Saveiro had **more than 44% of its segment** and ranked fourth among light commercials [S12].

**Fiat Strada / RAM 700.** Autocosmos (2010) already listed Strada among Mexico’s small pickups [S3]. For 2015, RAM launched a Brazil-built Strada rebadge as **RAM 700** (briefly shown as RAM 750 in some reports), with regular cab and Club Cab, from October 2014 / model-year 2015 [S2][S13]. Autocosmos 2020–2021 called RAM 700 the compact-segment leader after it arrived, then covered a full second generation as RAM 700 2021 (Fiat Strada elsewhere), on sale in Mexico from **18 November 2020** [S1].

**Renault Oroch** is the main classification dispute. Renault and some Mexican used-car press branded it a **“Sport Utility Pickup” (SUP)** sitting between Saveiro/RAM 700 and midsize work trucks such as Nissan NP300 or Mitsubishi L200 [S14]. Autocosmos nonetheless ran a 2020 comparison titled as a **compact pickup** duel between Oroch and RAM 700, and credited Oroch with opening a more “leisure” four-door unibody pickup sub-segment [S4]. Oroch reached Mexican dealers in **May 2018** [S15][S14]. This research therefore includes Oroch in compact-adjacent rankings from 2018, while recording that Autocosmos’s November 2020 “only three compact pickups” line **omitted Oroch** [S1].

**Not compact (contrast only).** Official and specialist pickup rankings for Mexico are dominated by **Nissan NP300** (and related Chasis/Estacas/Frontier splits), **Toyota Hilux**, **Ford Ranger**, **Mitsubishi L200** and full-size names. T21, citing INEGI, reported 193,332 pickup sales in 2019 and named NP300, Hilux, Ranger, L200 and Tacoma as 52.74% of all pickups — none of those five is the compact class defined above [S16]. Best Selling Cars Blog placed Chevrolet S10 (Colorado-related, Thailand-built) in 2015 as a **midsize** entry **between Tornado and Silverado/Cheyenne** [S17]. Peugeot Hoggar was presented as a Brazilian/Argentine small pickup, not as a Mexican retail product [S3].

**Nameplates by year (documented presence, not a sales rank):**

| Years | Documented compact / compact-adjacent nameplates in Mexico |
| --- | --- |
| 2010 | Tornado; Saveiro (continuous later coverage); Fiat Strada; Ford Courier (last year in the small-pickup set) [S3][S5][S6] |
| 2011–2013 | Tornado; Saveiro; Fiat Strada [S3][S8][S10] |
| 2014 | Tornado; Saveiro; Fiat Strada; RAM 700 from Oct (Strada rebadge) [S2][S10][S13] |
| 2015–2017 | Tornado; Saveiro; RAM 700 [S2][S11] |
| 2018–Oct 2020 | Tornado; Saveiro; RAM 700; Renault Oroch (SUP / compact-adjacent) [S1][S14][S15] |
| Nov–Dec 2020 | Same four; RAM 700 second generation on sale from 18 Nov 2020 [S1] |

The compact class **did not contain five simultaneous nameplates** in most years of 2010–2020. A “top 5 compact pickups per year” request therefore hits a structural limit: the honest list is usually **three**, and at most **four** once Oroch arrives.

## Open gaps / uncertainty

- No AMDA/AMIA glossary page was fetched that officially defines “pickup compacta” as a statistical bin; the definition used here is the Mexican specialist-press convention, which conflicts with INEGI/AMIA **all-pickup** tables.
- Ford Courier’s last Mexican model year is cited from secondary encyclopedic text, not a Ford de México circular.
- Exact calendar years when Fiat Strada left the Fiat price list versus RAM 700 arriving overlap in 2014; unit splits for that overlap year were not found in a primary table.
- Autocosmos (Nov 2020) said the compact class had three models; the same site compared Oroch as a compact pickup two weeks later. Renault’s SUP label is a real conflict, not a typing error.
- Direct INEGI RAIAVL model microdata for 2010–2014 compact nameplates was not downloaded in this session.
