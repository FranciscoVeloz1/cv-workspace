# q06: On cheap-but-reliable criteria (purchase price, parts, fuel, payload vs durability), which compact pickups look strongest as used buys, and what are their documented advantages and problems?

**Status:** researched — pending validation
**Source types to prioritize:** Combined primary specs + Mexican TCO/parts coverage + used-market evidence

## Findings

“Cheap but reliable” in this class, using **only sourced facts**, splits into: (1) **lowest 2026 residual**, (2) **simplest mechanicals / fuel**, (3) **payload**, (4) **parts/dealer coverage implied by sales volume**, (5) **safety kit**. No Mexican TCO study comparing these four nameplates was fetched.

### 1. Cheapest used: Chevrolet Tornado

Guía Autométrica (Aug 2026) puts 2015 Tornados around **$120,000–$135,000** and 2018s **$148,000–$171,000** [S1][S2]. That is the **lowest documented residual** in this project. Advantages documented by GM/press: **105 hp 1.8 + five-speed manual only**, **734 kg** payload (highest among the compact figures sourced), claimed **~16 km/l**, dual airbags and ABS on later trucks, Chevrolet commercial dealer network [S3][S4]. Problems documented: **no automatic**; Motorpasión listed **2020 Tornado without ESC** [S5]; the model **faded in sales** (8,034 in 2011 [S6] → 5,246 in 2018 → 3,717 in 2019 → 2,808 in 2020 [S7][S8]) and was on the way out by 2020 [S9]. Lower late-cycle volume means **thinner used selection of healthy late trucks** and, eventually, weaker new-parts continuity than Saveiro/RAM 700, which still sell. That last point is an inference from sales, not a parts-catalog citation.

### 2. Volume + residual “safe compact”: RAM 700 (first gen) / Saveiro

RAM 700 led compact **unit sales** in 2015–2018 and 2020 [S8][S10][S11]. Autocosmos repeatedly calls it compact **segment leader** after arrival [S12]. Documented advantages: 2015 **1.6/115 hp**, 650–705 kg, ABS/airbags, 7-year/100,000 km warranty when new, Club Cab uniqueness, front locker on Adventure/Club [S13][S14]; 2021 gen added ESC and better connected radio [S12]. Used asks in this session: **~$205,000** (2019, 155,000 km) to **~$254,000** (2020, 73,000 km) [S15] — **more money than a guide Tornado**. Problems: first-gen **no ESC** on Motorpasión’s 2020 list [S5]; **2021** Strada/RAM 700 **one-star Latin NCAP** [S16]; Autocosmos 2014 drive says the 1.6 wants **high rpm** [S13]; FWD leaf-sprung chassis; 2021 engine is **98 hp** [S12], i.e. **less power** than the 2015 115 hp 1.6.

Saveiro: VW claimed **44% compact share** in 2014 and again **>44%** in 2019, plus #1 in its segment and a TCO claim in the 2019 commercial-vehicle release [S17][S18]. 2017 range started at **$182,000**, 101–110 hp 1.6, 5-speed, 607–712 kg, discs/ABS/airbags across the 2017 range; Cross had ESC [S19]. 2019 it **out-sold RAM 700** (8,890 vs 7,483) [S8]. Autocosmos 2021 still calls it a durability/efficiency example [S20]. Guía Autométrica (Aug 2026) puts 2017 Starline at **$132,200** and Cross at **$216,200**; 2020 Robust **$161,900** / Pepper **$241,600** [S23]. Problems: base trucks on the 2020 **no-ESC** list [S5]; double-cab payload **falls** vs regular cab [S19].

### 3. “More truck-like cabin / only automatic”: Renault Oroch

Only compact-adjacent model with a **factory automatic** (4-speed) and a **140 hp 2.0**, 6-speed manual alternative, five-seat SUV cabin, **650 kg**, **710 kg** tow in the 2020 Autocosmos compare, multilink rear [S21][S22]. 2019 sales **7,527**, second among the small set [S8][S22]. Guía Autométrica 2018–2020: about **$176,600–$219,700** by trim [S24]; classifieds on the 2019 guide page asked **$245,000–$339,000**. Problems: **dearest new** in 2018 ($270k–$329k) [S21]; Renault itself (via Seminuevos) says it is **not** an NP300/L200 [S22]; Autocosmos 2020 compare: **worse fuel** than RAM 700 2021 (16.1 / 14.8 vs 18.3 km/l), **weaker safety kit** than 2021 700 (two airbags, no ESC in that compare) [S21]; Motorpasión 2020 **no-ESC** list [S5].

### Cheap-but-reliable ranking **as the sources allow** (not a lab test)

| Priority | Stronger documented fit | Why (sourced) | Documented downsides |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lowest pesos now | Tornado 2015–2018 | Autométrica ~$120k–$171k [S1][S2]; simple 1.8/M5; 734 kg [S3] | No ESC (2020 spec) [S5]; dying sales [S7][S8]; no auto |
| Parts/volume + still cheap-ish | RAM 700 2016–2018 or Saveiro 2017–2019 | Years of compact sales leadership or 44% share [S10][S11][S18]; Saveiro work-cab guide ~$132k–$162k [S23] | Club Cab 700 costs more used than Tornado [S15]; ESC hole on pre-2021 700 and base Saveiro [S5] |
| Cabin/automatic | Oroch 2018–2020 | Only auto; 140 hp [S21] | Guide ~$177k–$220k but ads higher [S24]; fuel, SUP limits, no ESC [S5][S21][S22] |

**Reliable** in the mechanical-simplicity sense: Tornado and base Saveiro/RAM 700 are **small naturally aspirated fours and manuals** [S3][S13][S19]. **Reliable** in the crash-avoidance sense: **none** of the 2020 compact quartet was a Motorpasión ESC “buy” [S5]; the one later crash-tested nameplate (**2021 RAM 700**) scored **one star** [S16].

VW’s 2019 **TCO leadership** claim for Saveiro [S18] is the only manufacturer TCO line found; it is **not** an independent audit.
