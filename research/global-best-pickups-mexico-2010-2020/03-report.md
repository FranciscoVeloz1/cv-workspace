# Globally regarded pickups that were sold new in Mexico, 2010–2020

## Executive Summary

The trucks with a **world reputation** that a Mexican buyer could actually order new in 2010–2020 were not the compact unibody class (Tornado, Saveiro, RAM 700, Oroch). They were **Nissan’s NP300 family**, **Toyota Hilux**, **Ford Ranger**, **Mitsubishi L200**, **Toyota Tacoma**, **Volkswagen Amarok**, **Chevrolet Colorado**, a separate **Nissan Frontier V6**, the US full-size badges (**Ford Lobo / F-150**, **Chevrolet Silverado / Cheyenne**, **RAM Crew Cab / RAM 1500**, **GMC Sierra**, **Toyota Tundra**), and from mid-2019 **Jeep Gladiator**. **Isuzu D-Max** — a global durability icon — was not a Mexican new pickup until 2026. [1][2][13]

Among that set, **Mexican sales** are lopsided. Whenever INEGI-style model lines exist (especially **2017–2020**), **NP300 is first by a wide margin** and **Hilux is second**. Ranger, L200, RAM Crew Cab, or a GM/Ford full-size line then fill 3rd–5th depending on the year and on whether cab splits are combined. A complete top 5 of this set is **not documented** for 2010, 2012, 2013, or full-year 2014; 2011 and 2017 are only partial. [6][9][10][18][22]

The important catch: **the Mexican VIN is often not the Australian, Thai, or US hero truck**. The 2016 Hilux was petrol **2.7 / 166 hp / 5-speed / 4x2 only**. Diesel 4x4 arrived as **2018 model year**. Latin NCAP’s **5-star** Hilux is a **July 2019+** 7-airbag + ESC double cab, while a January 2020 Mexican round-up still listed **Hilux without ESC**. NP300’s volume sits beside **4 Latin NCAP stars**, two airbags on the tested truck, **optional ESC**, and **AMIS theft leadership**. [4][24][26][27][28]

For a used buy in 2026 guide pesos, **petrol NP300 and petrol Ranger XL** cluster near **$207,000–$227,000**; **Hilux petrol double-cab** is higher; **diesel 4x4 Hilux automatic** and **Ranger 3.2 4x4** sit roughly **$300,000–$360,000** on the guide; **Tacoma V6** is higher still. Those are Guía Autométrica / asking figures, not notarized sales. [30][31][32][33]

## Introduction

A previous study ranked Mexico’s **compact FWD unibody** pickups. This report answers a different question: which pickups with **international standing** (global sales, durability reputation, independent crash tests, or class-defining status) were **sold new in Mexico** in calendar years **2010–2020**, how they ranked **inside that set**, what engines Mexico actually got, and where global reputation **fails** on a Mexican used example.

**In scope:** the nameplates above. **Out of scope as “globally best”:** Tornado, Saveiro, Strada/RAM 700, Oroch (contrast only). Years after 2020 appear only to date the D-Max launch or current used guides for 2010–2020 trucks.

## Global icons that were (and were not) sold in Mexico

Autocosmos’s June 2019 midsize catalog is a snapshot of what dealers were actually offering: **Colorado, Ranger, L200, NP300, US-style Frontier, Hilux, Tacoma, Amarok**, with **Gladiator** due later that year. JAC Frison T6 is in that catalog but is treated here as a local Chinese midsize, not a world-class nameplate on the same evidence. [1]

**Toyota Hilux** is the durability brand: Autocosmos used the “indestructible” nickname; Toyota executives, quoted at the Mexican diesel launch, said more than **17.3 million** Hilux in **180+ countries**, Mexican arrival in **2003**, **13,644** sales in 2016 making Mexico the **10th** Hilux market, and almost **93,000** Mexican units since 2003. The eighth generation for Mexico came from **Thailand**. The United States and Canada do not sell Hilux; they sell **Tacoma**. [1][2][3][4]

**Nissan NP300** is Mexico’s work default. Autocosmos dated Datsun pickups in the country to **1959** and split work NP300 bodies (chassis, stakes, single/double cab) from plusher “Frontier” trims of the same family. Mexico is, in that catalog’s telling, the only market where that NP300 Frontier **and** a separate **US-style Frontier 4.0 V6** (2005-generation in the article) were sold together. [1]

**Ford Ranger** is the global T6 midsize. El Financiero repeated Ford’s claim of a 2013 J.D. Power “preferred small pickup globally” award — manufacturer/press, not a Mexican test. [1][7]

**Mitsubishi L200** is Triton elsewhere; Autocosmos called it a legend in other latitudes and noted FCA’s RAM 1200 / Fiat Fullback as other faces of the same truck. The **Mexican 2019** catalog was a much milder petrol/diesel 5-speed range. [1]

**Volkswagen Amarok** arrived in **2010 as model year 2011**, diesel-only and double-cab in the 2019 catalog. **Toyota Tacoma** is built in **Tijuana** and is the US-oriented sibling sold alongside Hilux. **Chevrolet Colorado** remained after work **S-10** versions were withdrawn; INEGI still recorded 48 S10 leftovers in 2018. [1][8][9]

Full-size North American trucks were retailed under Mexican badges: **Lobo** and **F-150**, **Silverado** and **Cheyenne**, **RAM Crew Cab / ST Crew Cab** and **RAM 1500**, **Sierra**, **Tundra**. **Jeep Gladiator** went on sale in July 2019 as Rubicon, **3.6 Pentastar / 285 hp / 8-speed automatic**, **$1,119,900**. Autocosmos counted **394** Gladiators in 2019 and **1,325** in 2020. [9][10][12][6]

**Not offered as a new Mexican pickup in 2010–2020:** Expansión reported Isuzu’s **first** Mexican pickup, the Thailand-built **D-MAX**, on **30 July 2026**, after two decades of trucks-only. Honda **Ridgeline** does not appear in the 2019 midsize catalog or in Motorpasión’s 2018–2020 INEGI model lists; Mexican press covered Alabama production without a fetched Mexican launch. Mazda **BT-50** was not found as a 2010–2020 Mexican retail line in those same lists. [13][1][9]

## Mexican engines, power, and transmissions

Mexico often sold the **badge** with a **narrower, weaker** catalog than the diesel 4x4 Hilux / Ranger / Triton known abroad.

**Hilux.** 2016 launch: Thailand-built **2.7 petrol, 166 hp, 180 lb-ft, 5MT, 4x2 only**, ABS and **three airbags**, from **$251,900** chassis to **$326,100** SR double cab. [4] Diesel **2.8 / 174 hp**, **310 lb-ft** (manual) and **332 or 334 lb-ft** (automatic — T21 vs Motorpasión), 6MT or 6AT, 4x4, from November 2017 as 2018 MY; fleet manual **$420,000**, automatic **$579,900**; still ABS + 3 airbags on those listed kits. Motorpasión noted that in most markets the requested Hilux is diesel. 2019 catalog payload **820–1,210 kg**. [24][2][1]

**NP300.** 2019 catalog: **2.5 petrol 166 hp / 178 lb-ft**; diesel **161 hp / 297 lb-ft**; **6MT** (petrol RWD; diesel 4x4); top **7-speed automatic**; payload **901–1,525 kg**. Motorpasión (2020): **two airbags** and ABS on all, ESC only on some. [1][5]

**Frontier V6.** **4.0 / 261 hp / 5AT**, payload **590 kg**, six airbags and ESC in the 2019 catalog. [1]

**Ranger.** 2.5 petrol **164 hp / 167 lb-ft / 5MT / 4x2**; 3.2 diesel **197 hp / 347 lb-ft / 6AT / 4x4**; fleet 2.2 diesel **150 hp / 284 lb-ft / 6MT / 4x4**. Payload about **1.27–1.3 t**. Autocosmos claimed **seven airbags + ESC from the cheapest** 2019 Ranger. 2020 prices: XL 2.5 **$380,900**; XLT 3.2 4x4 **$624,600**. [1][25]

**L200.** 2.4 petrol **126 hp / 143 lb-ft** RWD and a diesel **134 hp / 232 lb-ft** 4x4, **5MT** either way, payload **1,065 kg**, two airbags. Autocosmos’s “cuatro litros turbodiésel” wording is internally inconsistent with a 134 hp four-cylinder; the **power, torque, and 5MT** are what the catalog clearly states. [1]

**Tacoma.** **3.5 V6 278 hp / 265 lb-ft / 6AT**, 4x2 or 4x4, payload **682 kg**, built in Tijuana. 2016 launch listed **seven airbags** and Star Safety (ABS, VSC); 2019 catalog says **six**. 2016 MSRP **$452,100–$539,200**. [1][8]

**Amarok.** Diesel double-cab: 2.0 TDI **140 hp** and **180 hp**; a top engine at **224 hp / 272 lb-ft** that Autocosmos labeled “V6 de 2.0” — almost certainly a press error for the 3.0 V6, not a fact to “correct” without a ficha. ESC on dearer trims only in that catalog. Payload **1,151 kg** on the entry 4x2/4x4. [1]

**Colorado.** 2.5 **200 hp / 6AT / 4x2**; 3.6 **308 hp / 8AT / 4x4**; payload **695 kg**; six airbags + ESC. [1]

**Gladiator.** Only **3.6 / 285 hp / 8AT**; payload **720 kg** (Motorpasión) or ~725 kg (Autocosmos preview). [12][1]

## Reliability, safety, theft, problems

No Mexican PROFECO or J.D. Power pickup reliability ranking was fetched. Durability talk for Hilux remains manufacturer/press language. [2][1]

**Latin NCAP (21 August 2019).** Hilux Double Cab / SW4 from **Argentina and Thailand**, produced **from 1 July 2019** (VIN `8AJKX3CD9L2012000`), **5 stars** adult and child after adding **ESC and seven airbags**. Frontal/side tests in **2015** had also been five stars under the previous protocol. Nissan Frontier Double Cab / NP300 Navara Double Cab, produced in **Argentina and Mexico**, **4 stars**, **two frontal airbags + ESC**, structure **borderline unstable**, no pole test without side head airbags. Latin NCAP told buyers to insist on ESC. [26]

**Mexican ESC catalogs (January 2020).** Motorpasión listed models it said lacked ESC. The list includes **Mitsubishi L200**, **Nissan NP300 (optional on the whole range)**, and **Toyota Hilux**. **Ranger is not on that list.** That can sit beside the Latin NCAP Hilux if **base Mexican petrol Hilux** lagged the July 2019 updated double cab. [27]

**Theft (AMIS, insured vehicles).** 2019: **84,133** stolen; pickups **15,263** (18%). Top model: **NP300 4,657**. 2020 calendar year (Motorpasión): NP300 **2,731** stolen (second overall behind Versa); Hilux **548** appears in the violent-theft five. AMIS is insured theft only; NP300’s rank tracks ubiquity as well as targeting. [28][29]

**Payload vs image.** Lifestyle V6 midsizers in the 2019 catalog haul **590–695 kg** (Frontier V6, Tacoma, Colorado) versus NP300 up to **1,525 kg** and Ranger about **1.3 t**. [1]

## Best-buy ranking among globally regarded Mexican offerings

There is no single winner. The sources support **four different “bests.”**

1. **Cheapest globally badged work truck still everywhere:** **NP300** petrol chassis/SE. Guide ~$207k–$233k for 2018, huge body/parts mix, payload up to 1.5 t. Cost: theft leadership, two airbags, optional ESC, 4-star tested structure. Volume was also a **financing** story (Súbete), not only metal. [30][1][26][27][28][7]

2. **Closest to the world Hilux:** **diesel 4x4 Hilux, 2018 MY onward**, not a 2016 petrol 4x2. Confirm ESC/airbags against the **July 2019** Latin NCAP cutoff. Guide $284k (diesel MT) to $360k (diesel auto); ads higher. Still a violent-theft target in 2020 AMIS. [24][26][27][32][29]

3. **Global midsize with a better Mexican ESC/airbag catalog and 3.2 diesel torque:** **Ranger 3.2 4x4**. 2018 XLT guide **$303,500**, below Hilux diesel auto. Petrol 2.5 XL is a different, cheaper truck (~$208,000) — same badge trap as petrol Hilux. [25][27][31]

4. **US-style V6, ESC, Mexico-built, pay more, haul less:** **Tacoma** (~$375k–$419k guide). Colorado is the GM analogue (308 hp, 695 kg). Gladiator is a $1.1 million Wrangler pickup, not a work substitute. [33][1][12]

L200’s Triton reputation does not survive the Mexican **126/134 hp, 5MT, no-ESC** catalog as a “global best” used buy. Amarok is a low-volume diesel double-cab with ESC not on entry trims. Full-size Lobo/Silverado/RAM 1500 had real 2018–2020 volume but no fetched Mexican ficha or Autométrica table in this project, so they are not priced here. [1][27][9]

## Counter-case: the Mexican truck is not the Australian/US truck

- **Hilux 2016–2017 volume metal is petrol 4x2.** Diesel 4x4 is a late-decade extra. [4][24][3]
- **“Hilux is 5-star” overreaches** unless the VIN matches July 2019+ 7-airbag ESC double cab. Motorpasión still named Hilux among no-ESC Mexican models in January 2020. [26][27]
- **NP300 first in sales is not first in crash or theft outcomes.** Four stars, two airbags on the tested DC, optional ESC, AMIS #1 stolen in 2019. [26][5][28]
- **L200/Triton legend ≠ 126 hp Mexico.** [1]
- **Ranger petrol 2.5 is not the 3.2 everyone quotes.** [25]
- **Tacoma/Colorado/Frontier V6 can haul less than a work NP300.** [1]
- **D-Max was not a legal new-car answer** in this decade. [13]
- **Hilux is still stolen with violence** in AMIS’s 2020 violent five (548). Toyota sheet metal is not a cloak. [29]

## Synthesis / Conclusion

**What sources report:** Mexico’s globally famous pickups in 2010–2020 were NP300, Hilux, Ranger, L200, Tacoma, Amarok, Colorado, Frontier V6, the US full-size family, and late Gladiator. NP300 dominated sales; Hilux was the durable #2; Ranger and then L200 or RAM Crew Cab or a GM/Ford full-size filled the rest when tables exist. Mexican catalogs often sold petrol, fewer gears, less ESC, and less payload than the same names abroad. Independent crash and theft data punish **assuming the badge equals the export truck**.

**What the available evidence suggests** (inference, not a sourced fact): a buyer who wants “the best in the world, but the one Mexico actually sold” should **filter on nameplate first, then on engine, 4x4, ESC, and VIN date**. The cheapest globally badged used work truck is still **NP300**. The closest Mexican match to the world midsize diesel myth is **Hilux 2.8 4x4 (2018+)** or **Ranger 3.2 4x4**, with Ranger looking better on 2019–2020 ESC press catalogs and Hilux looking better on Latin NCAP **if** the truck is the updated double cab. Compact unibody volume leaders from the companion study remain a different, cheaper class.

## Limitations & Open Questions

- No complete top 5 of this set for **2010, 2012, 2013, full-year 2014**; **2011** and **2017** are partial.
- NP300 **2016→2017** is a reporting unification, not comparable YoY growth.
- **2020 third place** depends on whether Chevrolet Silverado cab lines are summed.
- Lobo vs F-150 and Silverado vs Cheyenne splits are inconsistent across Motorpasión and Autocosmos.
- Hilux automatic diesel torque **332 vs 334 lb-ft**; Tacoma **six vs seven airbags**; Amarok “V6 2.0”; L200 “cuatro litros” — press conflicts, not resolved by manufacturer PDFs.
- No Focus2move (or other) **annual world ranking** page was fetched; Hilux’s global standing is Toyota’s cumulative claim plus Autocosmos reputation language.
- No PROFECO reliability ranking; no fetched recall campaigns (a Tacoma recall headline was not opened).
- AMIS is **insured** theft. Later-year AMIS “percent violent” figures were not used.
- Autométrica is an **August 2026 guide** for 2018 trucks, not 2018-era sold prices. L200, Amarok, Colorado, and full-size guides were not fetched.
- Ridgeline/BT-50 absence is catalog/INEGI-list absence, not a proof of zero private imports.
- Latin NCAP protocol changes after 2019 that affect marketing of old stars were not fetched.
