# q01: Which globally regarded pickup nameplates were sold new in Mexico in 2010–2020, under what Mexican badges, and which famous trucks were not offered?

**Status:** researched — pending validation
**Source types to prioritize:** Manufacturer Mexico launches, Autocosmos catalogs, AMIA nameplate lists

## Findings

A June 2019 Autocosmos catalog of **midsize** pickups then sold new in Mexico listed Chevrolet Colorado, Ford Ranger, JAC Frison T6, Mitsubishi L200, Nissan NP300, Nissan Frontier (US-style V6), Toyota Hilux, Toyota Tacoma, and Volkswagen Amarok, with Jeep Gladiator described as arriving later in 2019 [S1]. Compact FWD unibody pickups (Tornado, Saveiro, RAM 700, Oroch) are outside this project’s “globally best” set and appear only as contrast.

**Toyota Hilux.** Autocosmos described the Hilux as having a global reputation as “la indestructible,” with an eighth generation whose first generation dated to 1968 [S1]. Toyota executives, quoted by T21 at the Mexican diesel launch, said Hilux had been sold in more than 180 countries with more than 17.3 million units, arrived in Mexico in 2003, and that Mexico’s 13,644 sales in 2016 made it the 10th Hilux market worldwide, with almost 93,000 Mexican units since 2003 [S2]. Manufactura/Notimex likewise dated Mexican arrival to 2003 and said the 2018 diesel double-cab 4x4 versions were Toyota’s first diesel vehicles in Mexico, built in Thailand; the United States and Canada were described as not selling Hilux because they sell Tacoma instead [S3]. Autocosmos’s August 2015 launch piece said the 2016 Hilux for Mexico came from Thailand [S4].

**Nissan NP300 / Frontier (Mexico-built work truck) and Nissan Frontier V6 (US-style).** Autocosmos called NP300 the undisputed midsize-segment volume leader, with Datsun pickups in Mexico since 1959, and distinguished work NP300 bodies (chassis, stakes, single/double cab) from plusher “Frontier” trims of the same family; it also stated that Mexico is the only country where NP300 Frontier and a separate US-oriented Frontier (4.0 V6, launched 2005 in that article’s telling) coexist [S1]. Motorpasión’s 2019-sales safety analysis repeated the work NP300 vs NP300 Frontier split [S5]. Autocosmos’s 2020 pickup table still listed NP300 and a separate “Nissan NP300 Frontier” line (409 units in 2020), i.e. the V6 US-style truck remaining a distinct INEGI/press line [S6].

**Ford Ranger.** The 2019 Autocosmos catalog treated Ranger as the global T6 midsize, grown to about 5.3 m, with petrol and diesel catalogs in Mexico [S1]. El Financiero, citing Ford, reported a 2013 J.D. Power award as the preferred small pickup globally versus Toyota, Chevrolet, Nissan, and Volkswagen competitors — a manufacturer/press claim, not an independent Mexican test [S7].

**Mitsubishi L200 (Triton globally).** Autocosmos said the L200 is “considerada una verdadera leyenda” in other latitudes, also known as Triton, and that FCA’s RAM 1200 / Fiat Fullback were other faces of the same truck; the Mexican 2019 L200 was the pre-facelift petrol/diesel 5MT range [S1].

**Volkswagen Amarok.** Autocosmos dated arrival to 2010 as model year 2011, diesel-only and double-cab only in that 2019 catalog [S1].

**Toyota Tacoma.** Autocosmos said Mexico is essentially the only market where Hilux and Tacoma are sold together, and that Tacoma is built in Mexico [S1]. Autocosmos’s August 2015 Tacoma 2016 launch said the third-generation truck was already on sale in Mexico, produced at Toyota’s Tijuana, Baja California plant [S8]. Manufactura noted Tacoma (not Hilux) as the North American Toyota pickup [S3].

**Chevrolet Colorado (and residual S10).** Autocosmos said work S-10 versions had been withdrawn, leaving Colorado as a more recreational double-cab, priced even above some Silverado versions [S1]. Motorpasión’s 2018 INEGI-based ranking still recorded 48 Chevrolet S10 units in 2018, i.e. a leftover line rather than an active volume product [S9].

**Full-size North American nameplates sold in Mexico.** INEGI-based Motorpasión year lists for 2018–2020 include Ford Lobo and Ford F-150 (sometimes split, sometimes combined), Chevrolet Silverado and Chevrolet Cheyenne, RAM Crew Cab / RAM ST Crew Cab, RAM 1500, GMC Sierra, and Toyota Tundra [S9][S10][S11][S6]. These are the Mexican badges of the US full-size class. Autocosmos’s 2020 dedicated pickup table further splits Silverado/Cheyenne/Sierra/Lobo by cab and lists Ford F-250/F-350/F-450 Super Duty work trucks [S6].

**Jeep Gladiator.** Autocosmos (June 2019) said national arrival should be late 2019 with a 3.6 Pentastar / 8-speed automatic [S1]. Motorpasión (16 July 2019) reported Gladiator on sale in Mexico as Rubicon only, 3.6 V6 / 285 hp / 8AT, $1,119,900 [S12]. Autocosmos recorded 394 Gladiator sales in 2019 and 1,325 in 2020 [S6].

**JAC Frison T6.** Listed in the 2019 Autocosmos midsize catalog as assembled in Hidalgo [S1] and present in 2019–2020 sales tables [S6]. It is a Mexican-market Chinese midsize, not treated here as a globally class-defining nameplate on the same evidence as Hilux/Ranger/F-Series.

**Globally famous trucks not offered new in Mexico in 2010–2020.** Expansión (30 July 2026) reported Isuzu’s **first** Mexican pickup as the Thailand-built D-MAX, after two decades focused on trucks/commercial vehicles, entering a midsize segment it described as dominated by Nissan, Toyota, Ford, Volkswagen, RAM, and GM [S13]. That launch is outside 2010–2020. Honda Ridgeline does not appear in Autocosmos’s June 2019 midsize catalog [S1] nor in Motorpasión’s 2018–2020 INEGI model lists reviewed for this question [S9][S10][S11]. Mazda BT-50 was not found as a Mexican retail nameplate in those same catalogs/lists; Expansión’s 2026 D-MAX story mentions Mazda selling the related truck as BT-50 under an Isuzu agreement, without documenting 2010–2020 Mexican BT-50 retail [S13].

## Open gaps / uncertainty

- No Focus2move (or other independent) **worldwide annual unit ranking** page could be fetched this session; global standing for Hilux rests on Toyota quotes (180+ countries, 17.3 million cumulative as of Nov 2017) plus Autocosmos reputation language, not a 2010–2020 yearly world-sales table.
- Honda Ridgeline absence is inferred from catalogs and INEGI-based lists, not from a manufacturer statement that it was never imported.
- Mazda BT-50 Mexican retail 2010–2020 was not documented; the 2026 Expansión mention is about the global Isuzu/Mazda relationship at D-MAX launch.
- Exact Mexican on-sale years for every full-size generation (which Lobo vs F-150 badge applied in 2010–2014) were not reconstructed from manufacturer press kits.
- JAC Frison is in the 2019 midsize catalog but is not treated as globally class-defining on the sources above.
