# q02: Among that set, what were the top 5 by Mexican annual sales for each calendar year 2010 through 2020?

**Status:** researched — pending validation
**Source types to prioritize:** INEGI RAIAVL, AMIA, AMDA, Motorpasión/Autocosmos year lists

## Findings

**Ranking rule used here.** The set is globally regarded nameplates sold new in Mexico (q01): Nissan NP300 family (including published splits such as Chasis Largo / Estacas / Doble Cabina / Frontier trims of that family), Toyota Hilux, Ford Ranger, Mitsubishi L200, Toyota Tacoma, VW Amarok, Chevrolet Colorado, Nissan Frontier V6, Ford F-150/Lobo, Chevrolet Silverado/Cheyenne, RAM Crew Cab / RAM 1500, GMC Sierra, Toyota Tundra, Jeep Gladiator. **Excluded:** compact FWD unibody (RAM 700, Saveiro, Oroch, Tornado) even when they outsell some icons. Figures are **as published** (AMIA/AMDA/INEGI via press). Combining NP300 splits or Lobo+F-150 is noted separately because it changes ranks. Press sources are secondary compilations of association/INEGI data.

### 2010
Transporte.mx, citing AMDA, reported **149,352** pickup units sold in 2010, after 154,724 in 2009, as the market recovered from the 2008 crisis [S1]. **No model-level top 5 among the global-regard set was located** for calendar 2010.

### 2011
Expansión, citing AMIA, named the pickups with the most sales as Nissan **Chasis Largo 21,062** and Chevrolet **Tornado 8,034** [S2]. Tornado is compact unibody and is excluded from this ranking. Hilux, Ranger, and other globally regarded nameplates were **not listed** in that AMIA pickup pair, so a complete top 5 of the global set for 2011 is not available from this source. Segment total that year: 138,713 pickups [S1].

### 2012
AMDA’s sexenal market report listed Nissan **Chasis Largo 25,220** (17% of its light-truck slice in that table) and Nissan **Pick Up DC 8,847** (6%) among 2012 leaders [S3]. That is not a full pickup ranking of the global set (Hilux/Ranger/full-size not shown in the extracted leader rows).

### 2013
No complete calendar-year model ranking for Mexican pickups in the global set was fetched.

### 2014 (January–July only)
AMDA’s July 2014 internal-market report, for a light-truck slice totaling 82,025 units in that window, listed: Nissan Chasis Largo **14,032** (17%); Nissan Pick Up DC **5,648** (7%); Nissan Estacas Largo **4,555** (6%); Toyota Hilux **4,452** (5%); Ford Ranger Crew Cab **3,835** (5%) [S4]. This is **year-to-date through July**, not a full 2014 calendar ranking.

### 2015
Autocosmos’s top-30 light vehicles (AMIA-style annual list) included these globally regarded pickup lines [S5]:

| Rank in all light vehicles | Line | Units |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | Nissan NP300 Chasis Largo | 31,700 |
| 22 | Nissan NP300 Doble Cabina | 12,424 |
| 25 | Toyota Hilux | 10,454 |
| 26 | Ford Ranger | 10,382 |
| 28 | Nissan NP300 Frontier | 10,143 |
| 30 | Nissan NP300 Estacas | 7,987 |

**Top 5 among the global set, treating published splits as separate lines:** (1) NP300 Chasis Largo 31,700; (2) NP300 Doble Cabina 12,424; (3) Hilux 10,454; (4) Ranger 10,382; (5) NP300 Frontier 10,143.

El Financiero, citing AMDA, said 2015 work-truck volume was **178,496** (+16.2% vs 2014, highest since 2008); Nissan Chasis Largo **31,700** and 18% share; Hilux third in that division at 6% share; Ranger Crew Cab fourth at 6%; Frontier fifth at 6%; Nissan Doble Cabina 7% [S6]. Autocosmos full-year Hilux 10,454 and Ranger 10,382 match those “6%” neighbors [S5]. TyT, citing AMIA, gave **January–November 2015** pickups 156,111 and YTD: Chasis largo 27,981; Doble cabina 11,431; Frontier 9,202; Ranger Crew Cab 9,136; Hilux 8,740 [S7] — consistent order, not full-year totals.

If NP300 splits are **summed** (researcher arithmetic from [S5]): 31,700 + 12,424 + 10,143 + 7,987 = **62,254**, then Hilux and Ranger are the next globally regarded names in the top 30; other full-size/midsize lines are not in that top 30.

### 2016
TyT, citing AMIA: **211,746** pickups (+17.6% vs 2015). Nissan Chasis Largo **23,517**; NP300 **23,357**; Frontier **14,526**; Hilux **13,644**. Nissan stopped reporting Chasis Largo and Frontier from October “por intereses propios,” per AMIA as quoted by TyT [S8]. Autocosmos top 30 [S9]: NP300 Chasis Largo **23,516**; NP300 Estacas **23,357**; NP300 Frontier **14,526**; Hilux **13,644**; NP300 Doble Cabina **10,698**. Autocosmos noted Ranger (and others) had dropped out of the top 30 versus 2015 [S9].

**Top 5 among the global set (published splits):** (1) Chasis Largo 23,516/23,517; (2) NP300 Estacas 23,357; (3) Frontier 14,526; (4) Hilux 13,644; (5) NP300 Doble Cabina 10,698. Ranger is not ranked because it is outside the top 30.

### 2017
Autocosmos: Nissan **NP300 75,324** (2016 comparison column **23,357**), with the explicit note that NP300 “ya no aparece por versión, sino unificadas” [S10]. Hilux **19,132** (2016: 13,644) [S10]. Motorpasión’s 2017 ranking repeats NP300 75,324 (+222.5%) and Hilux 19,132 (+40.2%) [S11]. The +222.5% is a **reporting unification**, not a tripling of demand — Autocosmos says so in plain language [S10]; TyT had already warned Nissan stopped splitting Chasis/Frontier in late 2016 [S8].

**Top 5 among the global set:** (1) NP300 75,324; (2) Hilux 19,132. Ranger and others are **not in the top 30** (30th is Toyota Hiace 8,918), so any third globally regarded pickup sold fewer than 8,918 units; exact 3rd–5th names are **not in these top-30 tables**.

### 2018
Motorpasión published a full INEGI model list [S12]. Among the global set, in published-line order:

1. Nissan NP300 — **67,052**
2. Toyota Hilux — **18,943**
3. Chevrolet Silverado — **7,892**
4. Ford Lobo — **6,201**
5. Ford Ranger — **5,900**

Next in the set: Mitsubishi L200 5,838; RAM Crew Cab 4,980; Toyota Tacoma 4,644; Ford F-150 3,124; Chevrolet Colorado 1,898; VW Amarok 1,851; GMC Sierra 1,524; Nissan Frontier V6 875; RAM 1500 377; Toyota Tundra 281 [S12]. Compact RAM 700 (9,797) and Saveiro (8,198) outsold Silverado but are excluded [S12]. If Lobo and F-150 are combined (6,201 + 3,124 = 9,325), combined Ford full-size would rank 3rd, ahead of Silverado — that combination is **not** how INEGI listed them.

### 2019
Motorpasión INEGI list [S13] plus Autocosmos 2020 article’s 2019 comparison column [S14]:

1. Nissan NP300 — **62,989**
2. Toyota Hilux — **17,654**
3. Ford Ranger — **9,148**
4. RAM Crew Cab — **8,068**
5. Mitsubishi L200 — **6,665**

Then Tacoma 5,516; Ford Lobo 5,079; Chevrolet Cheyenne 5,077; Chevrolet Silverado 4,592; Ford F-150 3,920; Amarok 2,555; Colorado 1,578; GMC Sierra 1,297; Frontier V6 850; RAM 1500 430; Gladiator 394; Tundra 178 [S13][S14]. Autocosmos 2020 table’s 2019 Silverado/Cheyenne/Lobo splits differ slightly from Motorpasión’s combined Silverado/Cheyenne/Lobo lines (e.g. Cheyenne DC 4,816 vs Motorpasión Cheyenne 5,077) [S14][S13] — use Motorpasión for 2019 all-vehicle ranks and Autocosmos for pickup-specific splits.

### 2020
Autocosmos dedicated pickup table: **138,831** pickups of 949,353 light vehicles [S14]. Among the global set, **as published (not combining cab splits):**

1. Nissan NP300 — **44,577**
2. Toyota Hilux — **13,666**
3. Ford Ranger Crew Cab — **6,560**
4. RAM ST Crew Cab — **6,114**
5. Mitsubishi L200 — **5,134**

Then Tacoma 4,784. Compact RAM 700 (8,011) would be 3rd of **all** pickups but is excluded [S14].

**Conflict if Silverado cabs are summed:** Autocosmos Silverado regular cab 3,726 + double cab 3,188 = **6,914**, which would outrank Ranger 6,560 [S14]. Motorpasión’s 2020 all-vehicle list has a single Chevrolet Silverado line at **7,193** and Ford Ranger 6,560, and combines “Ford F-150 / Lobo” at 5,990 [S15]. So 2020 3rd place among the global set is **Ranger** if one refuses to combine GM cab splits, or **Silverado** if one sums those splits / uses Motorpasión’s combined Silverado line.

## Open gaps / uncertainty

- Complete top-5 among the global set is **not sourced** for calendar years **2010, 2012, 2013, and full-year 2014**. 2011 names only Chasis Largo among the global set (plus excluded Tornado). 2017 names only NP300 and Hilux inside the top 30.
- NP300 2016→2017 jump is a **series break** (unification), not comparable YoY growth [S8][S10].
- 2018–2020 Ford Lobo vs F-150 and GM Silverado vs Cheyenne vs cab splits: ranks change if lines are combined. 2020 3rd place is the main conflict (Ranger vs summed Silverado).
- Transporte.mx “last year” ~180,000 is not labeled with a calendar year on the fetched page; 2010/2011 totals are explicit [S1].
- Raw INEGI RAIAVL microdata was not downloaded; all annual ranks are press compilations.
