# q04: What do reliability, crash tests, recalls, theft, and commonly reported failures show for these Mexican-market trucks?

**Status:** researched — pending validation
**Source types to prioritize:** Latin NCAP, PROFECO, specialist press, manufacturer campaigns

## Findings

Independent Mexican **reliability surveys** (PROFECO IQS-style, J.D. Power Mexico pickup IQS) were **not fetched**. What follows is crash-test, ESC-fitment, and insured-theft evidence, plus press safety catalogs. US J.D. Power/Consumer Reports for Tacoma/F-150 are out of scope unless labeled; they were not used.

### Crash tests (Latin NCAP, 21 August 2019)

Latin NCAP’s own release: Toyota **Hilux Double Cab / SW4** manufactured in **Argentina and Thailand**, produced **from 1 July 2019** (VIN `8AJKX3CD9L2012000` onward), **renewed 5 stars** adult and child under the then-latest protocol after adding **ESC and 7 airbags** as standard (three frontal, two side, two curtain). Frontal/side tests from **2015** under the previous protocol had also reached five stars; Toyota updated equipment and retested including pole and ESC [S1].

Nissan **Frontier Double Cab / NP300 Navara Double Cab**, produced in **Argentina and Mexico**, achieved **4 stars** adult and child. Equipment cited: **two frontal airbags and ESC as standard**. Structure rated **borderline unstable**; lack of standard side head protection meant no pole test; child score limited by 3-year-old dummy protection and lack of passenger-airbag cutoff [S1]. Latin NCAP recommended buying only cars with ESC [S1].

These results describe **the tested specifications and plants**, not automatically every cheap Mexican petrol Hilux or work NP300 (see q07 and the ESC list below).

Motorpasión’s March 2020 safety round-up of 2019 bestsellers said Latin NCAP gave NP300 **4 stars** for adult and child protection, and that Mexican NP300s have **two airbags**, ABS, and ESC only on some versions [S2] — aligning with the 4-star Navara/Frontier test on airbags, while noting ESC is **not** on every Mexican NP300.

### Electronic stability control on Mexican 2020 catalogs

Motorpasión (8 Jan 2020) listed vehicles it said lacked ESC, following Latin NCAP’s “do not buy without ESC” advice. The list includes **Mitsubishi L200**, **Nissan NP300 (optional across the range)**, and **Toyota Hilux**, as well as compact pickups Tornado, RAM 700, Saveiro, Oroch [S3]. **Ford Ranger is not on that no-ESC list** [S3]. That sits beside Autocosmos 2019 claiming seven airbags + ESC from the cheapest Ranger [S4] and Motorpasión Ranger 2020 describing side airbags on all versions [S5].

Conflict to keep explicit: Latin NCAP’s 5-star Hilux is the **July 2019+** 7-airbag+ESC Double Cab/SW4 [S1], while Motorpasión’s January 2020 Mexican-market list still names **Hilux** among models without ESC [S3]. Those can both be true if base Mexican petrol Hilux lagged the Latin NCAP-updated double cab.

Autocosmos 2019: Colorado and Tacoma standard ESC; Amarok ESC only on dearer trims; NP300 ESC on top Frontier; L200 no ESC in the listed kit [S4].

### Theft (AMIS — insured vehicles)

**2019:** Indicador Automotriz reprinting AMIS: 84,133 insured vehicles stolen; pickups 15,263 (18%). Top stolen model: **NP300 4,657** (−28.7% vs prior year; frequency 17.11), ahead of Tsuru 4,311 and Versa 3,803 [S6].

**2020 calendar year (Motorpasión / AMIS):** top stolen were Versa 3,661; **NP300 2,731**; Aveo 2,440; Tsuru 2,363; Vento 1,867. Among **violent** theft counts, **Toyota Hilux 548** appears in the five-model violent list (with Sportage, CX-5, Ibiza, Río) [S7]. Motorpasión does not give Hilux’s share of its own thefts that were violent in that article — only the 548 violent-theft count among the named five [S7].

NP300’s theft rank tracks **volume** (Mexico’s best-selling pickup; q02) as well as targeting; the sources do not isolate a theft-per-1,000-on-road rate except AMIS “frecuencia” 17.11 for NP300 in 2019 [S6].

### Common mechanical failures / recalls

No PROFECO complaint ranking or manufacturer recall campaign list for these Mexican pickups was fetched this session. Autocosmos’s Tacoma 2016 launch page linked related stories including a Toyota recall of 36,000 Tacoma (headline only in “recomendados”; the recall article itself was not fetched) [S8]. Durability reputation for Hilux remains manufacturer/press language (“indestructible,” 17.3 million global units) [S4][S9], not a Mexican failure-rate study.

### Payload vs “global work truck” image

Autocosmos 2019 payloads: NP300 901–1,525 kg; Ranger up to 1,270 kg; Hilux 820–1,210 kg; L200 1,065 kg; Amarok 1,151 kg; Colorado **695 kg**; Tacoma **682 kg**; Frontier V6 **590 kg** [S4]. Gladiator 720 kg [S10]. Lifestyle V6 midsizers carry less than the work NP300/Ranger diesel.
