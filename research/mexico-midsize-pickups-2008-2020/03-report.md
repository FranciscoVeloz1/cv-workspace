# Globally best midsize pickups sold new in Mexico, 2008–2020

## Executive Summary

Among ladder-frame midsize (one-tonne) pickups that were **sold new in Mexico** from 2008 through 2020, the sourced class leader every year is the **Toyota Hilux**. It is the nameplate with the widest documented global reach in this set: more than **17.7 million** cumulative sales by 2017, sale in **over 180** countries, and **521,000** units in calendar 2017 [1], plus the most country-level #1 finishes among all models in Best Selling Cars Blog’s 2017 and H1 2019 tallies [2][3].

That is **not** what Mexico bought most. Nissan’s **NP300** work family led pickup volume throughout the window (for example **44,577** units in 2020 versus **13,666** Hilux) [4]. Compact FWD trucks (**RAM 700**, **Saveiro**) also outsold several global midsize nameplates in 2019–2020 [4]. Those trucks fail a **global class** test.

For a used-truck buyer in Mexico in 2026, the evidence-based shortlist among 2008–2020 metal is: **Hilux** (especially late-2019 5-star double cabs and the 2018-on 2.8 diesel), **Tacoma** (Tijuana-built, sold here since 2005, seven airbags on MY2016), **Amarok** (ESC from the 2011 Mexican launch), **Ranger T6** only with a later airbag/ESC pack—not the 2013 XL/XLT launch spec—and **L200** diesel 4x4 for abuse. **Isuzu D-Max**, **Mazda BT-50**, and **Mercedes-Benz X-Class** were globally relevant one-tonne trucks that were **not** sold new in Mexico in 2008–2020 [5][6][7].

## Introduction

This report answers: which midsize pickups were **globally class-leading** in 2008–2020 **and** actually sold new in Mexico? “Best” means documented international standing (one-tonne reach, independent crash tests, dependability studies)—not cheapest used and not Mexican sales rank. Full-size F-150/Lobo, Silverado/Cheyenne, and RAM 1500 are excluded even when they dominate global pickup **volume**, because they are a different class [8][4].

Audience: a used-truck buyer shopping Mexico in 2026. Citations were retrieved for this project; the validation pass re-checked the figures the ranking leans on.

## What “midsize pickup” meant in Mexico

Official statistics do not publish a stable “mediana” codebook. INEGI’s light-vehicle cargo group includes **pickups with gross vehicle weight up to 6,530 kg**, which mixes compact work trucks, global midsize, and some full-size nameplates sold as light vehicles [9].

Mexican press in the window used **“pick up mediana”** for the ladder-frame, roughly one-tonne class: Nissan NP300 / Frontier, Toyota Hilux, Ford Ranger, Mitsubishi L200, Chevrolet Colorado, plus Volkswagen Amarok and Toyota Tacoma [10][11][12]. Autocosmos reported that midsize trucks were **62%** of the pickup segment at the close of H1 2015 and estimated **74%** by 2020 [12].

**In class (ranking candidates when sold new):** Hilux, Tacoma, Ranger (T6 from 2013), L200, Amarok (from MY2011), Colorado (from MY2013), and the finished **NP300 Frontier** (Navara-related), not the work estaquita.

**Wrong class for this ranking:** Chevrolet Tornado; RAM 700 (Fiat Strada–based FWD) [13]; Volkswagen Saveiro (FWD 1.6) [14]; Renault Oroch. **Full-size / HD:** F-150/Lobo, Silverado, Cheyenne, RAM 1500, Sierra, Tundra, Super Duty, Gladiator [4].

## Mexican sales as context, not the ranking

Public, official **model-level top-5 lists do not exist for every year 2008–2020**. INEGI RAIAVL monthly PDFs for 2018–2020 are brand totals [15]. What follows is AMIA/AMDA/INEGI as republished by Autocosmos, T21, TyT, Nissan, and others.

Pickup volume peaked at **217,667** in 2007, then **154,724 (2009)**, **149,352 (2010)**, **138,713 (2011)**, recovering toward ~**180,000** around 2015 [16]. Later: **211,746** (2016) [17]; **184,439** (2018) and **193,332** (2019) [18]; **138,831** (2020) [4].

Pinned model figures:

- **2008:** Nissan Chasis largo **25,782**; pickup leader in that Autocosmos AMIA recap. Complete midsize 2nd–5th not published [19].
- **2013:** Chasis Largo **24,994** [20].
- **2014:** NP300 **57,449** (Nissan year-end) [21].
- **2015:** NP300 family summed from Autocosmos top 30 ≈ **62,254** (comparison article **62,164**); Hilux **10,454**; Ranger **10,382**; RAM 700 **8,159** [22][10].
- **2016:** Reporting break—Nissan stopped publishing Chasis Largo and Frontier from October “por intereses propios” [17]. Do not add split rows naively.
- **2017:** NP300 **75,324**; Hilux **19,132**; RAM 700 **9,815** [23].
- **2018:** NP300 **67,052**; Hilux **18,943**; RAM 700 **9,797** [24]; T21: Ranger **5,900**, L200 **5,838**, Tacoma **4,644** [18].
- **2019:** T21 ladder-frame cut: NP300 **62,989**, Hilux **17,654**, Ranger **9,148**, L200 **6,665**, Tacoma **5,516** (**52.74%** of 193,332 pickups) [18]. Autocosmos also ranks Saveiro **8,890** and RAM 700 **7,483** above L200/Tacoma [4].
- **2020:** NP300 **44,577**, Hilux **13,666**, RAM 700 **8,011**, Saveiro **6,957**, Ranger Crew Cab **6,560** [4]. Next in-scope: L200 **5,134**, Oroch **5,065**, Tacoma **4,784**, Amarok **1,707**, Colorado DC **1,129**, NP300 Frontier **409** [4].

Nissan said **151,755** NP300 units in **2011–2014** combined [25]. Volume leadership is not class leadership.

## Global class leaders versus what Mexico sold

Full-size F-Series still sit at the top of **global pickup unit** charts; they are out of scope here.

**Hilux** is the documented one-tonne leader on reach: Toyota’s own 50th-anniversary accounting [1] and BSCB’s country-#1 lists (estimated **34** national poles in 2017, **18** if estimates are dropped; estimated **37** in H1 2019, **21** confirmed). Many 2017 wins were low-volume African markets; even the confirmed subset still put Hilux first [2][3]. Worldwide midsize **unit** league tables (Focus2move-style) were not retrieved.

**T6 Ranger** (Melbourne-engineered, built for “180 countries”) was, in October 2011, the **first full-chassis pickup to score five Euro NCAP stars** (89% overall, 96% adult, 81% pedestrian). ANCAP adopted that rating; PX II (2015–2022) remains five stars **based on 2011 tests**, with dual frontal, side-chest and side-head airbags and ESC on the **rated** variants [26][27]. The **outgoing** Ranger had a **two-star** adult ANCAP rating in 2008. GoAuto then scored global HiLux and Triton **four stars**, D40 Navara **three stars**, and the then-Colorado/D-Max generation **three stars** with twin fronts [26].

**Mexican T6 (2013) did not match that pack.** Autocosmos: the new midsize Ranger **finally** arrived after ACE 55 delays that had forced Ford to abandon introduction plans; XL/XLT 2.5 gasoline, **no airbags or ESC** (“should at least be optional”) [28]. Latin NCAP’s Ranger **+3 airbags from December 2019** is **four stars for adult occupants**, stable shell, ESC yes, **no side-head airbags**, pole test not run [29]. Mexican 2013–2018 Rangers are not “the world’s safest ute.”

**Amarok** was the first body-on-frame ute to receive **five ANCAP stars** (February 2011) from the **same crash** Euro NCAP scored **four stars** [30]. Mexican MY2011 launch included **ABS, ESC, and an electronic locker** from **$319,000** [31].

**L200 / Triton:** global four-star in that 2011 snapshot [26]; Autocosmos cited **3.3 million** worldwide and 37 years at the fifth-generation Mexican launch [12]. TyT documented a Mexican gasoline/diesel L200 in October 2012 [32].

**Tacoma** is the North American midsize, sold in Mexico **since 2005** (Tijuana; 2006 TDR Sport 4.0 V6 / 236 hp, payload **675 kg**, **$299,900**) [33][34]. El Economista (2014): about **7%** of Tijuana Tacoma output stayed in Mexico [35]. MotorTrend, citing J.D. Power’s **2026 US Vehicle Dependability Study** of **2023** original owners, calls the previous-generation Tacoma (**2016–2023**) the most reliable US midsize truck—**not** a Mexican 2008–2015 survey [36].

**Colorado** reached Mexico as MY2013 (on sale 27 August 2012): 3.6 V6, 6AT, ESC and dual fronts on that launch [37]. 2018 GM México catalog: 2.5 **200 hp** / 3.6 **308 hp**, payloads **652–695 kg** [38]. Low 2019–2020 volume [4].

**Not sold new in Mexico, 2008–2020:**

- **Isuzu D-Max** — Expansión: Isuzu’s **first Mexican pickup** in July 2026; the company said D-MAX is sold in **120 countries** [5].
- **Mazda BT-50** — Mazda de México: **first pickup in almost 20 years** of local history; showrooms February 2025 [6]. (GoAuto treated BT-50 as the T6 Ranger’s mechanical twin globally [26].)
- **Mercedes-Benz X-Class** — sold from 2017, production ended May 2020; **15,300** global units in 2019; **not assembled in North America**; Argentina project cancelled [7]. Motorpasión México (2017) could not confirm Mexican availability [39].

## Mexican-market engines, horsepower, transmissions

Figures below are Mexican press kits and tests, not US/AU catalogs.

**Nissan NP300 (work, 2012 sheet):** gasoline KA24DEN **143 hp / 154 lb-ft**, 5MT RWD; diesel YD25DDTi **131 hp / 224 lb-ft**, 5MT, 4x2 or part-time 4x4. Payload **1,490 kg** (gasoline chassis) down to **865 kg** (diesel double cab 4x4) [40]. **NP300 Frontier** tester (2016): 2.5 **158 hp / 172 lb-ft**, 6MT RWD, payload **1,096 kg** [10]. Guía Autométrica 2016 still listed work lines as TM5 and some double cabs as TM6 [41].

**Toyota Hilux:** eighth-gen Mexican launch 2.7 Dual VVT-i **166 hp / 180 lb-ft**, 5MT, **4x2 gasoline only**; payload **820 kg** in the 2016 three-truck test—the lowest of that group [42][10]. Diesel MY2018: 2.8 **174 hp**; **310 lb-ft** with 6MT, **334 lb-ft** with 6AT; table prices **$420,900 TM / $579,900 TA**. An embedded tweet on the same page says **175 hp** and swaps those prices—treat the table and 174 hp body as the article’s own figures [43].

**Ford Ranger:** 2013 T6 MX 2.5 iVCT **164 hp / 167 lb-ft**, 5MT, payload up to **1,420 kg**, **$284,000 / $336,300** [28]. By the 2017 XLT diesel 4x4 test: 2.2 **122 hp / 223 lb-ft** and 3.2 I5 **197 hp / 347 lb-ft** 6AT 4x4; tester payload **1,095 kg**; about **9 km/l**; Argentine-built; quoted prices **$523,150 vs $532,150** unresolved [11].

**Mitsubishi L200:** 2016 fifth gen gasoline 2.4 **126 hp / 143 lb-ft**, diesel 2.5 **134 hp / 232 lb-ft**, 5MT either way; **$309,900 / $379,900**; payload **1,065 / 1,040 kg**; Thai-built [12]. 2012 Mexican range: payload up to **1,025 kg**; maker claimed frontal airbags and a rear child-seat anchor unique in segment then [32].

**Chevrolet Colorado (2018 catalog):** 2.5 **200 hp / 191 lb-ft** 6AT 4x2; 3.6 **308 hp / 275 lb-ft** 8AT 4x4; length **5,403 mm**; payload **652 / 695 kg** [38]. 2013 launch used a 3.6 **236 CV** 6AT with higher quoted payloads (**1,046 / 958 kg**) [37]—do not mix those years.

**Volkswagen Amarok:** 2011 2.0 bi-turbo **163 hp / 400 Nm**, 6MT [31]. 2018 Highline: 2.0 **180 hp / 295 lb-ft** or 3.0 V6 about **224–225 hp / 368–369 lb-ft**, **8AT and 4Motion only**; list **$650,000 / $780,000**, V6 tester **$825,000**—hp, torque, and price conflict across 2018 pages [44][45][46].

**Toyota Tacoma MY2016:** 3.5 V6 **278 hp / 265 lb-ft**, 6AT; US 2.7 **not** offered in Mexico; TRD **$452,100–$539,200**; seven airbags + VSC [47][33].

**Saveiro 2017 (not midsize):** 1.6 **101 hp** (Cross **110**), 5MT FWD, from **$182,000** [14]. **RAM 700:** Motor1’s **2021** Latin-American write-up lists **84 / 98 hp** FWD 5MT—that is not a 2015–2020 Mexican catalog [13].

## Reliability, safety, and known problems

Independent tests distinguish **global-spec** trucks from **Mexican decontented** trucks.

**Hilux:** Autocosmos 2016 test treated it as the durability/tow winner and the least comfortable empty [10]. Motorpasión’s 2023 used guide still recommends **2017–2019** gasoline or turbodiesel, with a warning about **high-speed stability** [48]. Latin NCAP (21 August 2019): Hilux Double Cab / SW4 **five stars** adult and child under the then-latest protocol; trucks from **1 July 2019**, VIN `8AJKX3CD9L2012000`, have **ESC and seven airbags** standard. Earlier 2015-protocol tests were also five stars [49]. GoAuto 2011 still had global HiLux at **four stars**, with ESC then standard only on selected facelift variants [26].

**Ranger T6:** world-class crash architecture in 2011 [26][27]; Mexican 2013 launch **without airbags/ESC** [28]; June 2013 XLT test: no airbags, XL without ABS per the tester [50]; 2017 XLT diesel: **three airbags**, rear drums, ESC not listed as equipped [11]; Latin NCAP from December 2019: **four stars adult**, stable shell, still **no side-head airbags** [29].

**L200:** useful diesel/4x4; noisier cabin [10]; two airbags typical on 2016 MX versions [51]; Motorpasión 2023: preferred at a Zacatecas mine for abuse [48]. No Latin NCAP result for the exact Mexican 2016 diesel was fetched.

**NP300 work vs Frontier:** Latin NCAP tested **Frontier / NP300 Navara double cab + 2 airbags** (Mexico/Argentina): **four stars**, ESC yes, **bodyshell borderline unstable**, no standard side-head airbags [49][52]. El Poder del Consumidor via Autocosmos on **2021** volume NP300: **no ESC, no SRI, two airbags** [53]. Motorpasión: work NP300 got **ESC from 2019** on some chassis/cab versions [48].

**Tacoma MY2016 MX:** seven airbags, ABS, TRAC, VSC [47][33]. US J.D. Power result as above [36].

**Amarok:** ESC from 2011 Mexican launch [31]; 2018 four airbags + ESC [45]; Autocosmos 2018: SUV-like composure versus rivals that feel unstable above 120 km/h; most expensive midsize, overlapping full-size money [46].

**Colorado 2018 catalog:** StabiliTrak, four-wheel discs, curtain airbags [38]. Payload is the capability limit versus one-tonne rivals.

Forum/Opinautos tallies were not used. No PROFECO complaint dump or NHTSA recall compilation was built for this project.

Guía Autométrica reprints on Autocosmos, updated **August 2026**, are **guide values**. Classifieds on the same pages run **higher**. Early years often have no Autométrica row.

**2016 guides:** Ranger XL DC **$173,300** (cheapest among these 2016 rows); NP300 roughly **$184,300–$203,600**; L200 gasoline **$190,000**, diesel 4x4 **$220,500**; Hilux **$191,900–$237,100**; Colorado **$251,000–$294,200**—the expensive outlier [54][55][41][56][57]. Hilux 2016 listings on the guide page: **$295,000** and **$329,000** [54]. NP300 2016 DC listing **$310,000** [41].

**2020 guides:** NP300 about **$238,200–$264,900**; Hilux gasoline into the high **$200ks–$300ks**, diesel 4x4 AT leather **$414,500–$417,100** [58][59]. Asking examples for 2020 NP300 DC about **$339,000–$370,000** [58].

## Top 5 per year (global class among trucks sold new in Mexico)

Ranks are **nameplate standing**, not sales. A truck enters when Mexican **new-sale** is sourced. Years with fewer than five sourced class trucks are **left short**—padding with NP300 would break the rule.

**How the order was set:** Hilux #1 on Toyota reach + BSCB country wins [1][2][3]. From 2013, Ranger T6 is #2 as the **global crash platform**, with the Mexican-spec caveat in the cell—not as if every XL were a five-star ANCAP truck. Tacoma is the NA midsize actually sold here from 2005, with a far better Mexican safety pack than 2013 Ranger [33][28]. Amarok is the ANCAP five-star / Euro NCAP four-star ute with ESC on the Mexican 2011 launch [30][31]. L200 is the global four-star Triton sold here by 2012 [26][32]. Colorado is an also-ran (low volume, modest payload on the 2018 catalog) and stays **out of the five** once the others occupy the slots.

### What fails the test

- **NP300 work / Chasis / Estacas:** Mexican volume queen [10][4]; 2012 143 hp gasoline / 131 hp diesel [40]; 2021 volume row without ESC [53]. Not a global NCAP/BSCB class leader.
- **RAM 700, Saveiro, Tornado, Oroch:** compact FWD or subcompact [13][14][8].
- **F-150/Lobo, Silverado, RAM 1500:** full-size [4][8].
- **D-Max, BT-50, X-Class:** globally in the one-tonne conversation; **not sold new in Mexico 2008–2020** [5][6][7].

## Synthesis / Conclusion

The available evidence suggests a used buyer in Mexico who wants the **best midsize truck of that era**, not the most common one, should start with **Hilux**, then **Tacoma** or **Amarok** depending on whether they want NA V6/safety pack or European diesel/ESC-from-launch, then a **Ranger** only if the specific truck has the later restraint/ESC equipment, then **L200** diesel 4x4. That ordering is inferred from Toyota’s cumulative/geography data, independent crash programs, and Mexican catalogs—not from a single worldwide midsize sales table, which was not retrieved.

What sources **report**: Mexico’s pickup market in 2008–2020 was an NP300 market with compact FWD trucks in the mix [4][18]. What this report **concludes**: those volume leaders are the wrong answer to “globally best.” The Mexican Ranger is the sharpest trap in the class-leader story—same T6 nameplate as the 2011 five-star ute, often a decontented gasoline double cab [26][28][29].

## Limitations & Open Questions

- **2008–2012 class lists have fewer than five rows** because T6 Ranger, Colorado, Amarok (before 2011), and year-dated L200 were not sourced as Mexican new trucks yet. Complete **sales** top-5s for 2009–2012 were not found.
- **L200 2008–2011** Mexican new-sale is implied by 2016 predecessor pricing and “two generations in Mexico” catalog copy, not launch-dated here.
- Pre-T6 Ranger **new** in Mexico: Autocosmos 2013 reads as a T6 introduction. An aggregator (VERcity) listing 2008 Mexican Ranger sales was **not** used (source-quality bar). Compact NA Ranger, if sold, was a two-star ANCAP predecessor globally [26]—not a class-leader slot.
- No Focus2move-style global **unit** ranking of Hilux vs Ranger vs D-Max vs L200.
- BSCB country-#1 counts include **estimates**, especially in Africa [2][3].
- J.D. Power Tacoma result is **US 2023 MY** in a 2026 study [36].
- No Autométrica for Tacoma/Amarok; asking prices are individual ads; no Libro Azul.
- No PROFECO/NHTSA recall compilation; no Latin NCAP for Mexican 2016 L200.
- q01 findings still contain a leftover “cheap-but-reliable” sentence; this report follows the global-class plan instead.
- Amarok V6 hp/torque/price and Ranger 2017 tester price conflicts left unresolved [11][44][46].
- Colorado 2013 launch specs (Conduciendo) vs 2018 GM catalog are different trucks/years [37][38].
- RAM 700 2015–2020 Mexican engines were not fetched as a catalog.
