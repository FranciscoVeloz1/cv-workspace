# q06: Which midsize pickups were globally regarded as class-leading in 2008–2020, and which of those were actually sold new in Mexico?

**Status:** researched — pending validation
**Source types to prioritize:** Global midsize/one-tonne sales, ANCAP, international awards/press; Mexican launch coverage

## Findings

“Class-leading” here is **not** Mexican volume. Full-size F-Series/Silverado/RAM 1500 dominate **global pickup unit sales** in North America and are out of this project’s midsize scope (see q01). Among **ladder-frame one-tonne / midsize** trucks, independent crash programs, Toyota’s own cumulative-sales accounting, and country-level bestseller counts pick a short list. Only some of that list was sold **new in Mexico** in 2008–2020.

### Global one-tonne class: who the sources actually name

Toyota’s Hilux 50th-anniversary site (TMC data) records **more than 17.7 million** Hilux sold worldwide **by 2017** since 1968, sale in **over 180 countries and regions**, and **521,000** units in calendar **2017**; production in **six countries as of 2017** (18 countries historically) [S1]. Best Selling Cars Blog’s country-#1 tallies (not worldwide volume) put Hilux first among all nameplates: an estimated **34** national #1s in **2017** (**18** confirmed if estimates are dropped; 22 of the 34 in low-volume African markets) and an estimated **37** in H1 **2019** (**21** confirmed) [S2][S3]. BSCB also notes Hilux has been the most prolific country winner “for almost fifteen years” as of 2017 and “over fifteen years” as of 2019 [S2][S3]. That is the strongest **global midsize/one-tonne** volume-and-reach case in the fetched set. Focus2move-style global model tables were not retrieved (Cloudflare blocks in earlier passes); **do not** treat snippet “508k/550k Hilux” figures as sourced.

Ford’s **T6 Ranger** (Melbourne-engineered, built in Thailand/South Africa/Brazil “for 180 countries” per GoAuto) was, in **October 2011**, the **first full-chassis pick-up to score five Euro NCAP stars** (89% overall, 96% adult, 81% pedestrian—GoAuto: best pick-up score then and highest pedestrian score Euro NCAP had given any vehicle). ANCAP adopted that rating for Australia/NZ [S4]. ANCAP’s PX II page (on sale Sep 2015–Jul 2022) still gives **five stars**, based on **2011 tests**, with dual frontal, side-chest and side-head airbags and ESC standard on the **rated** variants; AEB/LSS from April 2019 production [S5]. GoAuto contrasted that with **outgoing Ranger two-star adult ANCAP in 2008**; global **HiLux and Triton four-star**; **D40 Navara three-star**; **Colorado / D-Max / Patrol / 70-Series** then **three-star** with twin fronts only [S4]. BSCB recorded Ford Ranger with **five** country #1s in 2017 (**three** confirmed) and **four** markets in H1 2019 [S2][S3].

**Volkswagen Amarok** was the **first body-on-frame ute** to receive **five ANCAP stars** (Feb 2011), from the **same crash** Euro NCAP scored **four stars** (stricter child-protection / seatbelt-reminder rules) [S6]. Carsales: Australian Amaroks had four airbags (twin front + head-protecting side) **standard**; GoAuto: Amarok **five stars in Australia when side/head airbags fitted**, **four stars globally** where base trucks had only twin fronts; ESC standard on Ranger/BT-50/Amarok then, only on **selected** HiLux facelift variants [S4][S6].

**Mitsubishi Triton / L200:** GoAuto 2011 called Triton a **global four-star** [S4]. Autocosmos, launching the **fifth generation in Mexico (MY2016)**, cited **37 years** and **more than 3.3 million** L200 sold worldwide [S7].

**Isuzu D-Max** is a genuine global one-tonne rival: BSCB **seven** country #1s in 2017 (from two in 2015; five confirmed) and **six** confirmed in H1 2019 [S2][S3]. Expansión (30 Jul 2026): Isuzu’s Mexican D-MAX launch is the brand’s **first pickup in Mexico**; Watanabe said D-MAX is sold in **120 countries** [S8]. That **excludes D-Max from 2008–2020 Mexican new-sale ranking**.

**Mazda BT-50** shared the T6 Ranger program (GoAuto: same pedestrian/safety development) [S4] but was **not** a Mazda México product in the study window. Mazda de México’s own 2025 BT-50 launch copy: “**Por primera vez** en nuestros casi 20 años de historia… tendremos una pickup”; showrooms Feb 2025, preventa from 18 Nov 2024 [S9].

**Mercedes-Benz X-Class** (Navara/Frontier-based, sold from **2017**, production ended May 2020): El Universal reports **15,300** global units in **2019** vs ~**66,000** for the Nissan donor that year; executives **did not assemble it in North America**; an Argentina plant for South America **did not happen** [S10]. Motorpasión México (Jul 2017) said it would **not** be sold in the US and that Mexican availability was **unknown** [S11]. No fetched source documents a Mexican new-car launch in 2008–2020.

**Toyota Tacoma** is the **North American** midsize, not the Hilux. MotorTrend, citing J.D. Power’s **2026 US Vehicle Dependability Study** of **2023** original owners: previous-generation Tacoma (**offered 2016–2023**) is the **most reliable midsize truck** in that study [S12]. That is a US three-year dependability result, not a 2008–2015 Mexican survey. Tacoma **was** sold new in Mexico throughout the window (below).

### Which of those were sold new in Mexico, 2008–2020

**In the Mexican new-car set (ladder-frame midsize):**

- **Toyota Hilux** — continuous Mexican midsize rival in Autocosmos class copy; eighth-gen gasoline from MY2016, 2.8 diesel from MY2018 (q03). Global standing [S1][S2][S3] applies to the nameplate Mexico actually sold.
- **Toyota Tacoma** — Motorpasión: on the Mexican market **since 2005**; Tijuana-built; **>46,000** Mexican units by the MY2016 launch; US 2.7 I4 **not** offered in Mexico then [S13]. T21 (Oct 2005): Tacoma 2006 launch for Mexico, Tijuana plant, TDR Sport **4.0 V6 / 236 hp**, payload **675 kg**, on sale from **3 Nov**, **$299,900** [S14]. El Economista (Sep 2014): Tijuana Tacoma output **63,000 → 89,000** planned for 2015; **~7% stays in Mexico**, rest exported to the US [S15]. Autocosmos MY2016: **3.5 V6 278 hp / 265 lb-ft**, 6AT, seven airbags + VSC, **$452,100–$539,200** [S16]. T21/INEGI later lists Tacoma among 2018–2019 pickup lines (q02).
- **Ford Ranger T6** — Autocosmos (4 Mar 2013): the **new** midsize Ranger **finally** reaches Mexico after ACE 55 delays that had forced Ford to **abandon introduction plans**; **2.5 iVCT 164 hp / 167 lb-ft**, 5MT, XL/XLT double cab, payload up to **1,420 kg**, **$284,000 / $336,300**; **no airbags or ESC** on that launch pack (“should at least be optional”); 2.2/3.2 diesels, auto, and 4x4 still future [S17]. That is **not** the 2011 Euro NCAP five-star specification [S4][S5][S17]. Whether a **pre-T6** Ranger was sold **new** in Mexico 2008–2012 is **not** established by this launch copy (it reads as an introduction). Used 2011 Rangers in Mexican classifieds are not proof of official new-car sale.
- **Volkswagen Amarok** — Autocosmos (18 Aug 2010): **MY2011** Mexican launch, **2.0 bi-turbo 163 hp / 400 Nm**, 6MT, Startline 4x2 / Highline 4x2 / Highline 4x4; **ABS, ESC, electronic locker** on the three manuals; from **$319,000** (Highline **$389,900 / $439,000**) [S18]. That ESC-from-launch pack is closer to the global safety story than the 2013 Mexican Ranger [S4][S6][S18].
- **Mitsubishi L200** — TyT (17 Oct 2012): Mitsubishi México midsize L200 gasoline and diesel, payload up to **1,025 kg**; maker claimed it was then the **only** truck in its segment with **frontal airbags and a rear child-seat anchor** [S19]. Autocosmos MY2016 fifth-gen: **3.3 million** global units / 37 years; **same** 126 hp gasoline / 134 hp diesel as the predecessor, priced **$5,000 / $20,000** above it—so a Mexican predecessor existed immediately before 2016 [S7]. Autocosmos catalog copy later says Mexico has known **two generations** of this midsize pickup [S20]. Earliest **fetched** Mexican new-sale year in this pass is **2012** [S19]; 2008–2011 Mexican L200 new-sale is implied by the predecessor-price line and “two generations” but not year-dated here.
- **Chevrolet Colorado (GMT31XX / Thai-Brazilian)** — Conduciendo (30 Jul 2012): Colorado **2013** on sale **27 Aug 2012**, LT 4x2/4x4, **3.6 V6 236 CV**, 6AT Hydra-matic, payload **1,046 kg (4x2) / 958 kg (4x4)**, **$365,200 / $408,600**, imported from **Rayong, Thailand**; dual front airbags, four-wheel ABS, traction and ESC standard on that launch [S21]. GoAuto’s **2011 three-star Colorado** refers to the **previous** Australian/Holden generation, not this 2013 Mexican truck [S4][S21]. 2018 GM México catalog (q03) is a later, lower-payload 2.5/3.6 pack. Colorado is a **weaker global-US also-ran** in the 2011 GoAuto snapshot and a **low-volume** Mexican line in 2019–2020 Autocosmos tables (q02)—sold in Mexico from **MY2013**, not a 2008–2012 ranking candidate on fetched launch evidence.
- **Nissan NP300 Frontier (D23 / Navara-related finished truck)** — Autocosmos 2016 comparison treats **NP300 Frontier** as the finished midsize versus work NP300 (q01/q03). Latin NCAP Aug 2019 tested **Frontier / NP300 Navara double cab** (Mexico/Argentina): **four stars**, two airbags + ESC, **borderline unstable** shell (q04). That is Nissan’s **global** one-tonne cousin sold in Mexico from the mid-2010s refresh—**not** the work estaquita. Work **NP300 / Chasis Largo** is Mexico’s volume king (q02) and is **not** a global class leader in the crash/sales sources above.

**Out of Mexican 2008–2020 new-sale ranking (globally relevant but not sold new here, or wrong class):**

- Isuzu **D-Max** — first Mexican pickup **July 2026** [S8].
- Mazda **BT-50** — first Mazda México pickup **2025** [S9].
- Mercedes **X-Class** — no North American assembly; Argentina project cancelled; Motorpasión did not confirm MX sale [S10][S11].
- Compact FWD: Tornado, RAM 700, Saveiro, Oroch (q01).
- Full-size: F-150/Lobo, Silverado/Cheyenne, RAM 1500, Tundra, Super Duty (q01).

### Ranking implication

Among trucks **Mexico actually sold new**, the **global** order the sources support is: **Hilux** (reach + cumulative volume + later Latin NCAP 5★ double cab) → **T6 Ranger as a platform** (Euro NCAP 5★) **with a hard Mexican-spec caveat** → **Tacoma** (NA dependability / Tijuana midsize, sold in MX from 2005) → **Amarok** (ANCAP 5★ / Euro NCAP 4★, ESC on Mexican 2011 launch) → **L200/Triton** (global four-star 2011; 3.3 million by 2015) → **Colorado** (sold MX from 2013; 2011 global snapshot was three-star predecessor) → **NP300 Frontier D23** (Latin NCAP 4★, unstable-borderline structure). **Work NP300** leads Mexican **sales**, not this list. Year-by-year top fives are q07.

## Open gaps / uncertainty

- No fetched **official AMIA table** of Mexican new Ranger sales before 2013; Autocosmos 2013 reads as a first introduction of this midsize Ranger.
- L200 Mexican new-sale **before 2012** is implied (predecessor pricing, “two generations”) but not pinned to 2008–2011 with a launch article in this pass.
- Focus2move / global unit league tables for Hilux vs Ranger vs D-Max vs L200 were **not** retrieved; ranking uses TMC cumulatives, BSCB country wins, and NCAP—not a single worldwide midsize volume table.
- BSCB country-#1 lists include **estimates** (especially Africa); the confirmed subset still puts Hilux first [S2][S3].
- J.D. Power Tacoma result is **US 2023 MY** in a 2026 study [S12], not Mexican 2008–2015 second-gen Tacoma.
- X-Class: absence of a Mexican launch article is not a PROFECO “never imported” certificate; El Universal + Motorpasión are the fetched bound.
