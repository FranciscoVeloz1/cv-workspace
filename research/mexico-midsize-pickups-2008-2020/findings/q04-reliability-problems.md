# q04: What reliability reputation and recurring problems (engines, transmissions, rust, electrics, crash safety) are documented for the globally leading models sold in Mexico?

**Status:** researched — pending validation
**Source types to prioritize:** Latin NCAP, ANCAP, established MX/US/AU auto press, J.D. Power where platforms match

## Findings

Independent crash tests and Mexican press distinguish **global-spec trucks** from **Mexican decontented spec**. That gap matters more than forum anecdotes, which this file does not use.

### Toyota Hilux

Autocosmos’s 2016 three-truck test treated Hilux as the durability/solidity winner in the tow contest and the least comfortable empty, with the **lowest payload (820 kg)** versus Frontier 1,096 kg and L200 1,040 kg [S1]. Motorpasión’s 2023 used-truck guide still recommends **Hilux 2017–2019** gasoline or turbodiesel under $500,000, while warning of **high-speed stability issues** it calls endemic because of suspension and ride height [S2].

Latin NCAP (21 Aug 2019): **Hilux Double Cab / SW4** renewed **five stars** for adult and child occupants under the then-latest protocol. Trucks produced **from 1 July 2019, VIN 8AJKX3CD9L2012000**, have **ESC and seven airbags** standard (three frontal, two side body, two curtain). Earlier 2015 protocol tests had also been five stars; Toyota added equipment and retested pole/ESC [S3]. Autocosmos citing El Poder del Consumidor on **2021** bestsellers still lists **Hilux DC: ESC yes, SRI yes, 7 airbags, Latin NCAP 5★/5★** [S4].

GoAuto (2011), summarizing Euro NCAP-era rivals: **HiLux scored four stars** globally at that moment, with ESC then standard only on selected facelift variants [S5]. Drive/GoAuto later reported **August 2013 ANCAP upgrades to five stars** for some Australian 4x4 extra/single cabs—not automatically the same as every Mexican gasoline 4x2 [S5][S3].

### Ford Ranger (T6 / PX)

GoAuto (27 Oct 2011): the Australian-engineered T6 Ranger was **the first full-chassis pick-up to score five Euro NCAP stars**, 89% overall, 96% adult, 81% pedestrian; ANCAP adopted that rating. The **outgoing Ranger had a two-star adult rating in 2008**. Global HiLux and Triton were then four-star; D40 Navara three-star [S5]. ANCAP’s page for PX II (on sale Sep 2015–Jul 2022) says the **five-star rating is based on 2011 tests** and applies to PX II / PX III / Raptor; dual frontal, side chest and side head airbags and ESC standard on those rated variants; AEB/LSS from April 2019 production [S6].

Mexican 2013 T6 launch **did not match that global safety pack**. Autocosmos: XL/XLT arrived with ABS advertised, but **airbags and ESC were noted as absent** and “should at least be optional”; diesels/auto/4x4 were still future [S7]. The June 2013 XLT test: **no airbags on a truck Autocosmos said should have them**; XL lacked radio and, per the tester, ABS [S8].

By the 2017 XLT diesel 4x4 test: **three airbags** (two frontal + driver knee), ABS/EBD, **rear drums**, **no ESC/traction listed as equipped**, no reverse camera, tailgate **without a lock**, thirstier than Frontier, **most expensive midsize then** [S9]. Latin NCAP **Ranger + 3 airbags from Dec 2019**: **four stars** adult, **stable** bodyshell, ESC yes, **no side head airbags**, pole test not run [S10]. So Mexican Ranger safety lagged the 2011 “world’s safest ute” claim until late-2019 ESC cars.

### Mitsubishi L200

Autocosmos 2016 comparative: diesel/4x4 useful, payload close to Frontier, cabin noisier/cheaper than rivals [S1]. Motorpasión 2016: two airbags and ABS/EBD on both Mexican versions [S11]. Motorpasión 2023: L200 preferred at a **Zacatecas mine** for abuse versus Toyota/Ford/Chevrolet; **not every version has a full safety kit**; **narrower** than many rivals; turbodiesels cost more [S2]. Autocosmos 2015 launch drove the fifth-gen L200 at **Cozamin mine, Zacatecas** [S12]. GoAuto 2011 called Triton a **global four-star** then [S5]. A Latin NCAP result for Mexican-market L200 in 2008–2020 was **not fetched**.

### Nissan NP300 work vs Frontier / Navara

Latin NCAP (Aug 2019) tested **Nissan Frontier / NP300 Navara double cab + 2 airbags**, made in Mexico/Argentina: **four stars** adult and child; **two frontal airbags + ESC**; **bodyshell borderline unstable**; **no standard side-head airbags** (pole not run); no passenger-airbag cut-off [S13][S3]. That is **not** the work estaquita. El Poder del Consumidor via Autocosmos on **2021** volume NP300: **No ESC, no SRI, 2 airbags**, no Latin NCAP stars listed in that table [S4]. Motorpasión 2023: work NP300 got **ESC from 2019** on Mexican chassis/cab versions; **Frontier Platinum 2020** is the one it prefers for build quality and some safety [S2]. Nissan 2012 spec sheet lists side-impact bars and collapsible column, not airbag counts, for the work range [S14].

### Toyota Tacoma

Mexican MY2016: **seven airbags, ABS, TRAC, VSC, EBD** on all versions [S15][S16]. MotorTrend, reporting J.D. Power’s **2026 US Vehicle Dependability Study** of **2023** original owners: previous-generation Tacoma (**offered 2016–2023**) is the **most reliable midsize truck** in that study [S17]. That study is US owners and 2023 MY, not a Mexican 2008–2015 second-gen survey.

### Volkswagen Amarok

2011 Mexican launch: **ABS, ESC, electronic locker** even on early diesels [S18]. 2018 Motorpasión: ESC, traction, EDS locker, **four airbags** [S19]. Autocosmos 2018 test: SUV-like on-road composure vs rivals that feel unstable above 120 km/h; **most expensive in the midsize class**, overlapping full-size money [S20]. GoAuto 2011: Amarok **five stars in Australia when side/head airbags fitted**, **four stars globally** where base trucks had only twin fronts [S5].

### Chevrolet Colorado (Mexican catalog)

2018 GM México catalog: StabiliTrak, four-wheel discs, curtain/side/front airbags, reverse camera [S21]. Payload **652–695 kg** is a capability limit versus one-tonne rivals [S21]. GoAuto 2011 still listed the then-Colorado among **three-star, twin-front-airbag** utes in Australia—**before** the GMT31XX generation in Mexico [S5].
