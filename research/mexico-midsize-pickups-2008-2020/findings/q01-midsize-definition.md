# q01: Which nameplates counted as midsize pickups in Mexico in 2008–2020, and which full-size trucks must be excluded?

**Status:** researched — pending validation
**Source types to prioritize:** AMIA segment notes, manufacturer MX catalogs, Autocosmos/Motorpasión class guides

## Findings

Mexican official statistics treat pickups as light-duty cargo trucks, not as a “midsize” marketing class. INEGI’s vehicle-in-circulation methodology places **Pick Ups with gross vehicle weight up to 6,530 kg** in the light-vehicle (RAIAVL) cargo group; heavier cargo trucks sit in the heavy-vehicle register [S1]. That official bucket mixes compact work trucks, global midsize pickups, and some full-size nameplates sold as light vehicles.

Mexican auto press in the study window used **“pick up mediana”** for the ladder-frame, roughly one-tonne class that competes with Nissan’s work/Frontier trucks, Toyota Hilux, Ford Ranger, Mitsubishi L200 and Chevrolet Colorado. Autocosmos (April 2016) called the Nissan NP300 “the undisputed queen of midsize pickups in Mexico” and treated Hilux and Ranger as its nearest volume pursuers, with L200 as the diesel/4x4 alternative; it noted that Ranger would have been in the same comparison on volume [S2]. Autocosmos later placed Ranger 2017 in the midsize segment explicitly, contrasting it with full-size (“Full size”) trucks that are larger and thirstier, and with the compact Chevrolet Tornado, which it said is about as long as a midsize pickup from 20 years earlier [S3]. Autocosmos (July 2015) said that at the close of H1 2015, **62% of the pickup segment was occupied by midsize trucks**, naming L200, NP300 Frontier, Ranger and Hilux, and estimated midsize could reach **74% by 2020** [S4].

**Nameplates that belong in a Mexican 2008–2020 midsize / work-pickup universe (this project’s in-scope set):**

- **Nissan NP300 / “Estaquitas” / Chasis Largo / Estacas / Pick Up / Doble Cabina**, plus the more finished **NP300 Frontier**. Nissan’s 2016 press kit treats Pick Up, Estacas and Chasis as one NP300 family built at CIVAC, Morelos [S5]. Autocosmos 2020 still reports work NP300 and NP300 Frontier as separate sales lines [S6].
- **Toyota Hilux** (gasoline 2.7 and, from model-year 2018, 2.8 diesel) [S2][S7].
- **Ford Ranger** (T6 generation in Mexico; gasoline 2.5 plus later 2.2/3.2 diesels) [S3][S8].
- **Mitsubishi L200** (gasoline 2.4 and diesel 2.5, double cab) [S4][S9].
- **Chevrolet Colorado** (GM midsize; Autocosmos lists it among Ranger’s midsize rivals) [S3].
- **Volkswagen Amarok** (Argentine-built midsize diesel; Autocosmos dates Mexican arrival to about 2010 and treats Frontier/Ranger/Hilux as direct rivals) [S10].
- **Toyota Tacoma** (Tijuana-built; Motorpasión says it had been on the Mexican market since 2005) [S11].

**Smaller pickups that Mexican sales tables mix with “pick-ups” but are not global midsize ladder-frame trucks:**

- **Chevrolet Tornado** — Autocosmos uses it as the compact-size benchmark versus midsize Ranger [S3]. El Financiero (2017) still lists Tornado among work-truck share leaders [S12].
- **RAM 700** — Fiat Strada rebadge for Mexico from model year 2015 [S13]; Motor1 describes the 2021 refresh as a Latin American subcompact, Fiat Strada–based, front-wheel-drive [S14].
- **Volkswagen Saveiro** — Volkswagen México launched the 2017 compact pickup in July 2016 with a 1.6-litre engine and front-wheel drive [S15].
- **Renault Oroch** — appears in Autocosmos’s 2019–2020 pickup sales table [S6]; Motorpasión later treats it as a compact, Dacia-based dual-purpose truck, not a one-tonne midsize [S16].

**Full-size / heavy light-duty trucks to exclude from “midsize” rankings** (they appear in the same Autocosmos pickup tables): Ford F-150 / Lobo, Chevrolet Silverado and Cheyenne, RAM 1500 and RAM ST Crew Cab, GMC Sierra, Toyota Tundra, Ford F-250/F-350/F-450/F-550, RAM 2500/4000, Jeep Gladiator [S6]. INEGI’s 6,530 kg cap still includes some of these as light vehicles [S1]; exclusion here is by **segment**, not by the official GVW cutoff.

**Practical implication for this project:** Mexican buyers and AMIA/AMDA tables often mean “pickup” as a whole. A cheap-but-reliable ranking for 2008–2020 must include the NP300 family because that is what Mexico actually bought as the work/midsize default [S2][S5][S12]. A strict global-midsize list (Hilux, Ranger, L200, Colorado, Amarok, Tacoma, Frontier) is a second cut. Compact FWD trucks (Tornado, Saveiro, RAM 700, Oroch) are cheaper but are not the same product.

## Open gaps / uncertainty

- AMIA does not publish a stable public “mediana vs compacta vs full-size” codebook for 2008–2020; press usage is the working definition.
- Nissan reporting split (NP300 vs Frontier vs Chasis Largo vs Estacas) changed around 2016–2017, so “one nameplate” vs “four lines” is a data issue, not a product-class issue (see q02).
- Chevrolet S10 existed in some Mexican years (Autocosmos linked an S10 2016 launch from the L200 article) but does not appear in the 2019–2020 Autocosmos pickup tables the way Colorado does; year-by-year S10 availability was not fully mapped here.
- Jeep Gladiator (2020) is midsize-adjacent internationally but is a low-volume, expensive lifestyle truck in the Autocosmos table, not a cheap-reliable candidate.
